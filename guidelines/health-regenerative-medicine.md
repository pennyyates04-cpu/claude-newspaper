# Daily Health & Regenerative Medicine News Guidelines

Focus on regenerative, preventive, and root-cause approaches to health — especially areas
that go beyond simply prescribing medications.

## Prioritize

- **Regenerative medicine:** PRP, stem-cell therapies, exosomes, tissue regeneration,
  platelet-based therapies, biologics, peptides, and emerging cellular therapies
- **Preventive medicine & longevity:** maintaining health, preventing disease, optimizing
  aging, metabolic health, inflammation, mitochondrial health, and recovery
- **IV therapies & nutrient therapies:** IV vitamins, hydration therapies, micronutrients,
  NAD+, glutathione, electrolytes, and other emerging approaches
- **Nutritional and micronutrient science:** vitamins, minerals, amino acids, antioxidants,
  and controversial nutrients/compounds (including vitamin B17/amygdalin) — clearly
  distinguish evidence from claims
- **Nervous system science:** brain health, autonomic nervous system, vagus nerve,
  neuroplasticity, neuroinflammation, neurotransmitters, the gut-brain connection, sleep,
  stress physiology, and nervous-system regulation
- **Hormones & metabolic health:** thyroid, insulin sensitivity, glucose regulation,
  cortisol, reproductive hormones, and other systems involved in overall health
- **Exercise as medicine:** strength training, Pilates, yoga, mobility, cardiovascular
  fitness, recovery, sauna/cold exposure, and how exercise affects the brain and body
- **Gut health & microbiome:** the gut-brain axis, microbiome, inflammation, and nutrition
- **Emerging technologies:** wearable health tech, biomarkers, diagnostic testing,
  personalized medicine, biohacking, and new ways of measuring health
- **Clinical trials and new research:** especially human trials in regenerative medicine,
  longevity, nervous-system health, and preventive medicine

## How to present it

Give 3–7 genuinely interesting developments, not a huge list. For each story explain:

1. What happened / what researchers discovered
2. Why it matters
3. What the evidence actually shows
4. Whether it has been studied in humans
5. How promising it is
6. What we still don't know
7. Whether it is currently available clinically, or still experimental

Prioritize peer-reviewed research, clinical trials, major academic medical centers, and
reputable scientific organizations. Include the date of the research and link to the
original study when possible. Favor new discoveries and developments over textbook-level
explanations.

## The evidence-strength rule (non-negotiable)

Do not automatically dismiss alternative or emerging therapies simply because they are
outside conventional medicine. At the same time, never present an experimental treatment,
supplement, IV, stem-cell therapy, or wellness claim as proven simply because a clinic or
influencer promotes it. For controversial topics, explicitly label the evidence tier:

- **Strong evidence**
- **Promising but preliminary**
- **Mixed / uncertain evidence**
- **Little or no credible evidence**
- **Claims that have been disproven or raise significant safety concerns**

Flag when a finding is new enough that it could still change over the next few years.

## The goal

What is new in medicine and human health that focuses on preventing, repairing,
optimizing, or understanding the body — rather than simply treating symptoms with
medication? Be intellectually curious but scientifically rigorous.
