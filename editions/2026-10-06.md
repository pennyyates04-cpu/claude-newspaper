## AWS signs $1B+ chip-design deal aimed partly at speeding up SAP, Salesforce, and Workday

**What happened:** Amazon Web Services signed a multiyear chip-design licensing deal worth more than $1 billion with Synopsys, naming AWS the lead customer for Synopsys's expanding "application-optimized" silicon IP business — chip blueprints tailored to specific workloads rather than generic components. The deal also expands AWS's use of Synopsys's electronic design automation (EDA) software, and the two companies plan to collaborate on applying AI across chip-to-system engineering.
**Why it matters:** A stated goal of the new custom silicon is to boost performance for major enterprise apps running on AWS — SAP, Salesforce, and Workday were specifically named. Faster, cheaper infrastructure under the hood lets those vendors improve price-performance without touching their own application code.

**Sales angle:** Cloud infrastructure strategy is usually invisible to buyers, but "AI-optimized infrastructure" is becoming a selling point in enterprise RFPs. Prospects evaluating ERP or CRM systems may start asking what cloud and chip strategy underpins a vendor's performance claims.
**SDR takeaway:** EDA (electronic design automation) is the software used to design computer chips. This deal shows the AI infrastructure race moving down to the hardware level, not just chatbots and models — hyperscalers are now building custom silicon to run everyone else's software faster.
**Conversation starter:** "Have you looked at how your current systems' underlying cloud infrastructure affects performance as you add more AI features?"

## Schneider Electric to acquire PTC for $22.6 billion

**What happened:** Schneider Electric signed a definitive agreement to acquire PTC Inc. — maker of industrial design and product lifecycle management (PLM) software including Creo and Windchill — for $22.6 billion in cash, or $205 per share, a roughly 42% premium to PTC's prior closing price. It's the largest acquisition in Schneider's history. The deal is expected to close by Q3 2027, pending PTC shareholder approval and regulatory clearance.
**Why it matters:** Schneider, a major industrial energy-management and automation company, is betting that owning PTC's design and engineering software lets it sell a complete "digital thread" running from product design through the factory floor to energy operations — a direct play into the industrial AI and smart-manufacturing push.

**Sales angle:** Any manufacturing or industrial prospect using PTC tools is about to go through a vendor-ownership change — a classic moment when companies reassess their broader software stack, including ERP and financial systems that need to integrate with design and manufacturing data.
**SDR takeaway:** PLM (product lifecycle management) software manages a product's data from initial design through manufacturing and service. This deal shows industrial giants buying software companies outright rather than just partnering with them — consolidation in industrial tech is accelerating.
**Conversation starter:** "With PTC becoming part of Schneider Electric, has your team thought about how that might affect your design-to-ERP data flow down the line?"

## OpenAI introduces "Dots," persistent AI agents for business tasks

**What happened:** At its September 29, 2026 DevDay, OpenAI introduced "Dots" — persistent AI agents, powered by GPT-6 Astra, that keep working toward a stated goal across connected apps after a user steps away, rather than needing a new prompt for every step. OpenAI says specialized Dots can be given their own organizational identity, credentials, and business responsibilities, with early pilots in procurement, invoice processing, customer support, and contracting. Dots are reachable through ChatGPT, Slack, and Microsoft Teams, rolling out first to Pro and Business Premium users, with Enterprise availability controlled by workspace admins.
**Why it matters:** This pushes general-purpose AI further into territory ERP and CRM vendors already compete in — ongoing, autonomous task execution tied to real business systems, rather than one-off chat answers.

**Sales angle:** As AI agents with their own credentials start handling procurement and invoicing, prospects will increasingly ask how a given platform's AI fits (or conflicts) with tools like Dots — questions about data access, security, and "who owns this workflow" become live issues in a sales conversation.
**SDR takeaway:** This is "agentic AI" in practice — AI that acts on tasks over time, not just answers questions. A Dot can carry out a multi-step task over hours or days without a human prompting each individual step, which is a meaningful jump from a standard chatbot.
**Conversation starter:** "As more AI agents start touching financial processes like invoicing, how is your team thinking about which system stays the source of truth?"

## Dynatrace closes $915M acquisition of AI-observability startup Arize

**What happened:** Dynatrace completed its $915 million acquisition of Arize, an AI observability and evaluation platform, on October 1, 2026. The deal combines Arize's AI-native tracing and evaluation tools — built for monitoring models and agents — with Dynatrace's existing observability across applications, infrastructure, and business processes. ## HubSpot ships "Deal Progression" to recommend a rep's next move

**What happened:** The week of October 5, 2026, HubSpot shipped Deal Progression: Next Best Actions and Deal Plans inside Sales Hub. The feature reads the emails, calls, and meetings tied to a deal, then recommends next actions, drafts follow-up emails, and proposes CRM updates for a rep to approve — instead of requiring reps to dig through transcripts and notes themselves. **SDR takeaway:** "Next best action" tools are becoming standard in modern sales software — AI reads deal activity and tells a rep what to do next instead of the rep piecing it together manually. This affects how SDR work itself will change going forward.
