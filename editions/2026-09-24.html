Workday's $51 Billion Silver Lake Take-Private Talks Gain Momentum

Reports first surfaced in mid-August 2026 that private equity firm Silver Lake was in talks to take Workday private in a deal that could value the HR and finance software company near $51 billion, sending its shares sharply higher. On September 17, Workday shares rose again after a report — citing people familiar with the matter — said the financing effort for the deal is continuing, with growing optimism the transaction could still happen, even though no formal offer or terms have been announced. It would rank among the largest software buyouts ever, and it's being read as a test of whether fears that AI agents will erode traditional per-seat SaaS pricing have gone too far — a PE firm betting this big suggests real confidence in Workday's long-term value.

Sales angle
Workday is a top NetSuite competitor in HR and finance software; new private ownership could shift its product roadmap, pricing, or go-to-market as owners push for cash flow over growth — worth knowing if a prospect currently runs or is evaluating Workday.
SDR takeaway
"Take-private" means a public company is bought out and delisted from the stock market, so it no longer has to report quarterly earnings publicly — that generally gives new owners more room to restructure the business.

S&P Cuts Oracle's Credit Rating to One Notch Above Junk on AI Debt Load

On July 9, 2026, S&P Global Ratings downgraded Oracle's long-term issuer credit rating to BBB- from BBB — the lowest rung of investment grade — citing Oracle's roughly $250 billion in committed AI-datacenter spending and its heavy reliance on OpenAI, which accounts for about half of Oracle's $638 billion revenue backlog. Since the downgrade, Oracle's bonds have continued to widen, with its 10-year debt yielding around 6.5% — well above typical BBB-rated companies — as capital spending has pushed free cash flow sharply negative. Oracle, NetSuite's parent company, is financing its AI buildout largely with debt rather than existing cash flow, and rating agencies now treat its dependence on a single customer (OpenAI) as a real risk — a reminder that Oracle's headline cloud growth comes with real financial strain underneath it.

Sales angle
A useful, low-key data point if a prospect ever raises Oracle's overall financial stability or AI spending as a factor in choosing NetSuite — the downgrade is about Oracle's AI-infrastructure debt specifically, not NetSuite's operations or roadmap.
SDR takeaway
A credit rating measures how likely a company is to repay its debt; BBB- is the lowest tier still considered "investment grade" — one more notch down and Oracle's debt would be rated "junk," which typically raises borrowing costs.
Conversation starter
"Oracle just committed a quarter-trillion dollars to AI infrastructure and got its bond rating cut close to junk status — as your company thinks about its own growth investments, how do you weigh debt against expansion?"

McKesson Discloses Breach After ShinyHunters Claims Theft of Patient and Business Data

McKesson, the largest U.S. pharmaceutical distributor, discovered unauthorized access to third-party applications on August 25, 2026, and filed an SEC Form 8-K three days later disclosing the incident and data exfiltration. The extortion group ShinyHunters claims the stolen data includes patient names, Social Security numbers, Medicaid numbers, medical records, prescription and shipment data, employee information, Salesforce records, and internal communications; McKesson says its investigation is still in early stages and has not yet determined whether the incident is material to the business. A breach at a company this size touching both patient data and "Salesforce records" in the same incident shows how a single vendor-side compromise can expose sensitive customer/business data across multiple connected systems at once — not just one database.

Sales angle
Relevant for healthcare, distribution, and any prospect consolidating systems — a concrete, recent example to raise when discussing how data governance and system consolidation (fewer disconnected platforms) can reduce this kind of exposure.
SDR takeaway
Public companies must disclose material cybersecurity incidents to the SEC within four business days under rules adopted in 2023 — the Form 8-K is that disclosure, not necessarily a sign the breach is confirmed as serious yet.

Wholesale Distributors Are Adopting AI Selling Fast — But Losing Track of Core Sales Metrics

Phocas Software's "State of Sales in Distribution 2026" report, published September 22 and based on a survey of more than 100 wholesale distributors, found 49% are now using AI for selling. But basic sales-performance tracking lags well behind: 39% don't track forecasting accuracy, roughly a third don't measure win rates (32% for existing business, 34% for new business), and 27% don't measure customer retention. Wholesale distribution is one of NetSuite's core target verticals, and the report shows a real gap between adopting AI selling tools and having the underlying data discipline — tracked, centralized metrics — needed to actually use them well.

Sales angle
Distributors layering AI sales tools onto disconnected systems, without centralized reporting, are a strong fit for an ERP/CRM platform with built-in analytics — a concrete operational pain point to probe with prospects in this vertical.
SDR takeaway
"Win rate" (the share of quoted or proposed deals that close) is a basic sales metric — a prospect not tracking it usually can't reliably forecast revenue or measure whether new tools (AI included) are actually helping.

Revenue Teams Hit 100% AI Adoption, but Only 1 in 5 Call It Production-Ready

Salesloft's 2026 US Revenue Benchmark Report, based on a survey of 500 U.S. sales and revenue leaders published September 2, found AI use has reached 100% adoption among respondents — but only 20.6% describe their AI strategy as "production-ready" with measurable outcomes, while 28.2% remain stuck in the experimentation stage. The report defines maturity not by how many AI use cases a team has, but by whether AI is embedded in daily workflows and tied to measurable efficiency and revenue results. It's a useful corrective to headlines about AI "transforming sales" — nearly every team has adopted some AI tool, but most haven't moved past pilot mode to something that reliably moves revenue numbers.

Sales angle
When a prospect says "we already have AI," this data helps reframe the conversation around maturity, not adoption — production-ready AI usually needs clean, connected data across systems, which siloed point tools often lack.
SDR takeaway
"Production-ready AI" means the tool is embedded in daily workflow and tied to a measurable outcome — not just switched on or used occasionally; know the distinction when qualifying a prospect's AI claims.
Conversation starter
"I saw research showing basically every sales team now uses AI, but only about 1 in 5 call it production-ready — where would you say your team falls on that spectrum?"

Intuit Raises QuickBooks Online Prices Up to 70%, Leans on New AI Features to Justify It

For renewals on or after August 1, 2026, Intuit raised QuickBooks Online subscription prices across its Essentials, Plus, and Advanced plans — Plus rose from $99 to $140 per month (41%) and Advanced from $200 to $340 per month (70%), while Free, Lite, Ledger, and Simple Start plans were unchanged. Intuit says the increases come bundled with expanded capabilities, including Bill Pay Elite, industry-specific tools for construction and professional services, and enhanced reporting; existing annual subscribers keep their current rate until renewal, and some recent sign-ups get a six-month price-protection window. QuickBooks is the dominant small-business accounting platform and a common "graduation point" for companies outgrowing it into a full ERP — a steep hike on the Advanced tier narrows the cost gap with entry-level ERP systems and could accelerate that migration conversation.

Sales angle
Growing companies now paying $340/month for QuickBooks Advanced are a natural audience for a total-cost-of-ownership conversation against NetSuite, especially since both are now emphasizing AI-driven functionality.
SDR takeaway
Know the specific numbers — Advanced's jump from $200 to $340/month is the sharpest increase and hits the segment of QuickBooks customers closest to outgrowing the platform.
