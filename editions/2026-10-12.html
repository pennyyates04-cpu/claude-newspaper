OpenAI revenue shortfall rattles Oracle and the AI trade

On October 8, 2026, the Financial Times reported that OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue run rate was about $50 billion at the end of September — roughly $20 billion below the ~$70 billion figure that had circulated in recent weeks. Oracle, which supplies a large share of the computing infrastructure behind ChatGPT through its Stargate cloud deals, fell about 4.7% to roughly $136.82 by midafternoon trading. Nvidia, Broadcom, and Microsoft also declined. Part of the gap may reflect accounting differences — rival Anthropic counts revenue booked through cloud partners like AWS and Google Cloud, while OpenAI's figure reportedly does not — but the report still rattled investors already nervous about the scale of AI infrastructure spending commitments. Oracle has bet heavily on AI cloud infrastructure demand, with its stock price closely tied to confidence that customers like OpenAI can pay for the capacity Oracle is building. Any sign that a major AI customer's revenue is smaller than assumed raises questions about whether the broader AI infrastructure buildout is outrunning actual paying demand.

Oracle's cloud infrastructure revenue more than doubled — but free cash flow went negative

Oracle reported Q1 fiscal 2027 results (quarter ended August 31, 2026) on September 10, 2026. Total revenue rose 30% to about $19.3 billion. Cloud infrastructure (IaaS) revenue jumped 121% to $7.4 billion, while cloud applications revenue (SaaS, which includes NetSuite) grew 10% to $4.2 billion. Non-GAAP EPS was $1.92, up 30%. But free cash flow was negative by roughly $5 billion as Oracle keeps spending heavily on data centers; remaining performance obligations (contracted future revenue) rose to $664 billion. Oracle raised full-year guidance to at least $90 billion in total revenue. It's the clearest look yet at the tension driving Oracle's recent stock volatility: cloud infrastructure demand is real and growing fast, but Oracle is burning cash to build capacity ahead of when it gets paid — the same dynamic behind the OpenAI-driven selloff above.

SDR takeaway

Salesforce cuts 133 more jobs — its fourth Bay Area round in under a year

Salesforce filed WARN notices in early August disclosing that 133 employees across San Francisco (74 roles) and Seattle/Bellevue (59 roles, including at Tableau) would lose their jobs effective October 5, 2026. Neither filing states a reason. It follows at least three earlier rounds of Bay Area cuts over the past year, including a widely reported 2025 round where CEO Marc Benioff said AI agent tool Agentforce let the company cut its support organization from about 9,000 to roughly 5,000 people, saying "I need less heads." Salesforce is one of the most visible examples of a software vendor publicly tying headcount reduction to AI agent deployment — a narrative that shapes how executives at prospect companies think about AI's effect on their own staffing.

Deloitte forecasts a record $1.7 trillion holiday shopping season

Deloitte's annual holiday retail forecast, released September 10, 2026, projects total US holiday retail sales (November 2026 through January 2027) of $1.70–$1.71 trillion, up 4.0–4.8% from last year. E-commerce is forecast to grow fastest, 7.5–8.4%, to $316.1–$318.9 billion. Deloitte's model assumes disposable personal income grows 4.5–5.2% during the season, and flags continued value-seeking behavior — like brand switching and promotion use — even as overall spending rises. For any prospect in retail, e-commerce, or consumer goods, this is the baseline budget and inventory-planning assumption they're almost certainly already working against this quarter.

Sales angle
Faster e-commerce growth plus persistent promotion-driven, value-seeking consumers is a strong, timely hook for retail and e-commerce prospects to discuss inventory visibility, multi-channel order management, and margin tracking — all core NetSuite ERP value props during peak season.
SDR takeaway

New research: cold calling still beats AI-written prospecting emails nearly 6-to-1

A ValueSelling Associates survey of 153 sales leaders and reps, released September 1, 2026, found that cold calling remains the second most effective prospecting method (47% effectiveness), behind only client referrals, while AI-written personalized emails scored just 8%. The rankings are little changed from a comparable 2018 study. 46% of sales leaders said reps give up on cold calling too easily, and 39% said reps are afraid to pick up the phone. Despite years of AI-prospecting-tool hype, human-led, higher-effort channels are still outperforming automated ones by a wide margin — directly relevant to how SDRs should prioritize their own time.

Sales angle
This is a useful, evidence-based counterpoint when a prospect's team is over-indexed on AI-generated outbound email at the expense of live conversations — effectiveness data, not just opinion, backs prioritizing the phone.
SDR takeaway
AI tools can help with research and personalization, but the data says they shouldn't replace the phone call as a core prospecting channel — call reluctance, not lack of AI tooling, looks like the bigger performance gap.
