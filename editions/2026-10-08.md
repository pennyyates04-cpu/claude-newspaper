## Salesforce's Winter '27 release puts AI agents in charge of entire workflows

**What happened:** Salesforce's Winter '27 release reaches general availability on October 12. Its headline change is turning on Agentforce by default in every org with Agentforce access — the toggle that used to require manual setup is going away. Salesforce frames the shift as agents moving from answering one-off questions to running whole workflows end-to-end: qualifying pipeline, resolving service cases, booking appointments, and underwriting risk. The release also expands Agentforce Contact Center to more than 30 countries and adds autonomous voice-based scheduling that Salesforce says compresses a 15-minute booking call to seconds.
**Why it matters:** This is Salesforce betting its core product experience on AI agents handling full business processes rather than assisting a human doing them — a meaningfully bigger claim than most "AI copilot" features made over the past two years.

**Sales angle:** NetSuite prospects evaluating CRM or ERP-adjacent tools will increasingly expect AI that completes a process, not just drafts a reply — a higher bar than "we have a chatbot."
**SDR takeaway:** "Auto-enabled by default" matters because it means millions of existing Salesforce orgs get these agents turned on without an IT project — a different adoption model than opt-in pilots.
**Conversation starter:** "As vendors move from AI that answers questions to AI that runs whole workflows, has your team thought about which processes you'd actually want to hand off first?"

## Oracle's cloud infrastructure revenue more than doubled last quarter

**What happened:** Oracle's fiscal Q1 2027 results, reported September 10, showed total revenue up 30% year over year to $19.3 billion, beating analyst estimates. Cloud infrastructure (IaaS) revenue jumped 121% to $7.4 billion, while cloud applications (SaaS, which includes NetSuite) grew a steadier 10%. Remaining Performance Obligations — Oracle's contracted-but-not-yet-recognized revenue backlog — climbed to $664 billion. Oracle raised its full-year guidance to at least $90 billion in revenue and non-GAAP EPS of $8.10, while continuing to fund its AI data center buildout partly through new stock and debt issuance.
**Why it matters:** The results confirm Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, not NetSuite or Fusion applications, is currently the company's fastest-growing and most-discussed business — which shapes where Oracle's investment dollars and public narrative are headed.

**Sales angle:** Prospects sometimes ask "is Oracle still investing in NetSuite, or is it all AI infrastructure now?" — the applications segment's steadier 10% growth alongside IaaS's 121% is a useful, honest way to answer that.
**SDR takeaway:** RPO (Remaining Performance Obligations) is the dollar value of contracts already signed but not yet billed or recognized as revenue — a $664 billion RPO signals a very large forward order book, mostly tied to AI infrastructure deals.
**Conversation starter:** "Oracle's cloud applications side, including NetSuite, grew more slowly than its AI infrastructure business last quarter — curious whether that kind of growth mix comes up when your team evaluates vendor stability?"

## September jobs report misses badly, raising odds the Fed holds rates

**What happened:** The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on October 2 that U.S. employers added just 29,000 jobs in September, far below the roughly 84,000–95,000 forecasters expected. The unemployment rate rose to 4.2% from 4.1%, and July and August payrolls were revised down by a combined 60,000 jobs. Healthcare (+17,000), construction (+11,000), and manufacturing (+9,000) were the only notable sector gains. Markets responded by pricing in a roughly 77–80% probability that the Federal Reserve holds its benchmark rate steady at its late-October meeting, rather than raising it further.
**Why it matters:** A weakening labor market changes the calculus for business spending broadly — including software budgets — since it's one of the clearest signals of where the Fed and, by extension, borrowing costs and consumer demand are headed next.

**Sales angle:** A softening job market often makes finance leaders more cautious about new spending, but it can also push companies toward automation and efficiency tools — including ERP upgrades — as a way to do more with a smaller headcount.
**SDR takeaway:** The Fed's "hold" versus "hike" decision affects the cost of capital for every company your prospects are — higher rates generally mean a higher bar for new software investment to clear.
**Conversation starter:** "With the jobs numbers coming in soft last month, has your finance team talked about how that might shift priorities for new tech spending this quarter?"

## Adobe forecasts record $275 billion holiday online shopping season, driven partly by AI traffic

**What happened:** Adobe's annual holiday shopping forecast, released September 28, projects U.S. online sales of $275.1 billion between November 1 and December 31, 2026 — up 6.7% year over year and a new record. Adobe expects traffic to U.S. retail sites referred by AI tools like chatbots and AI search to grow roughly 130% year over year, with the sharpest spikes around Thanksgiving and Black Friday. The forecast is built from Adobe's analysis of more than one trillion visits to U.S. retail sites across 100 million SKUs and 18 product categories.
**Why it matters:** This is one of the most-cited holiday retail forecasts each year, and the AI-traffic figure is a concrete, measured data point — not just speculation — showing how AI tools are starting to reshape how shoppers find products.

**Sales angle:** Retail and e-commerce prospects are actively restructuring how they present product data so it's findable by AI assistants, not just search engines — a theme worth raising with any retail or consumer-goods account.
**SDR takeaway:** "AI-referred traffic" means visits to a retailer's site that originated from a chatbot or AI search answer rather than a traditional search engine link — a new category retailers are starting to track separately in their analytics.
**Conversation starter:** "Adobe's holiday forecast expects AI-referred shopping traffic to jump about 130% this season — is that something your team is already seeing show up in your own site data?"
