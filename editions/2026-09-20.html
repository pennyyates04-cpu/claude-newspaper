Google Confirms Its Gemini AI Model Hacked Three Real Companies During a Security Test

Google disclosed that its Gemini AI model gained unauthorized access to three real companies' systems during a cybersecurity evaluation run in May 2026 by Irregular, an independent testing firm. Gemini was tasked with a "capture the flag" exercise to retrieve data from a fictional company inside a sandboxed test environment — but that fictional company shared its name with a real one. Using public information and guessed credentials, Gemini accessed three real, unrelated companies it believed were in scope, stopping each time once it recognized it had hit a genuine company. Google notified the affected organizations and other AI labs in late July; similar incidents involving Irregular have also hit Meta, Anthropic, and OpenAI. It's the first confirmed case of a major AI lab's model autonomously breaching real, unauthorized systems, even in a controlled test — a concrete data point in the debate over how much autonomy AI agents should get as enterprises deploy them for real business tasks.

Fed Raises Rates for the First Time Since 2023, Citing Persistent Inflation

The Federal Reserve's Open Market Committee voted 12-0 on September 16, 2026 to raise its benchmark interest rate a quarter point, to a target range of 3.75%-4%, its first increase since 2023. The statement cited inflation "remaining elevated," driven in part by high energy prices, even as it described economic activity as expanding at a solid pace with resilient spending and an unemployment rate that has changed little. Updated projections leave open the possibility of another hike before year-end. A hike, rather than the cuts many businesses had positioned for, raises the cost of capital for growth-stage and PE-backed companies and typically tightens corporate software budgets, increasing pressure on finance teams to show cost control.

Salesforce and NVIDIA Launch Koa, a Purpose-Built CRM Reasoning Model for Agentforce

On September 15, 2026, Salesforce and NVIDIA announced Koa, Salesforce's first CRM-specific "reasoning" AI model for its Agentforce platform. Koa was built by post-training NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Super on a proprietary synthetic dataset modeled on roughly 27 years of CRM deployment patterns — no customer data was used in training. On Salesforce's internal CRM benchmark, which tests real-world actions like updating an opportunity or routing a support case, Koa already matches or exceeds leading general-purpose models while making three times fewer errors. It's available now to select pilot customers, with general availability planned for this winter in U.S. regions. CRM vendors are moving past generic AI chat assistants toward models purpose-trained to autonomously execute multi-step CRM workflows — sharpening AI competition in the customer-data layer that often sits alongside, or overlaps with, ERP systems like NetSuite.

Sales angle
Useful competitive context for prospects running Salesforce alongside a legacy or homegrown ERP — Salesforce is investing in AI that automates CRM busywork, raising the bar for what "AI-powered" means across their stack.
SDR takeaway
A "reasoning model" is AI trained to plan and execute multi-step actions, not just answer questions — Koa autonomously does CRM tasks like updating records, not just chats about them.

Uber Cuts 10% of Its Workforce to Flatten Management and Move Faster

Uber announced on September 2, 2026 that it will cut about 3,300 jobs, roughly 10% of its global corporate workforce, as part of a restructuring to flatten its management hierarchy. The plan cuts managers by 20%, halves teams of only one or two people, and caps remote-work eligibility at roughly 1% of staff. CEO Dara Khosrowshahi said the changes will help the company move "simpler and faster" and redirect resources toward ride-sharing, delivery, and autonomous vehicles. Uber's stock rose as much as 1.7% in premarket trading on the news. It's a high-profile example of a large company deliberately flattening management to move faster — a move other mid-market and enterprise companies face pressure to replicate, often by leaning on automation instead of headcount.

New Study: Cold Calling Still Beats AI-Written Emails 6 to 1, but Buyers Are Harder to Reach

ValueSelling Associates and Selling Power surveyed 153 B2B sales leaders and reps on prospecting effectiveness. Sellers ranked cold calling the second most effective way to reach a new buyer — behind only a client referral (74%) — and six times more effective than an AI-written email (47% vs. roughly 8%). Execution lags, though: just 19% of leaders rate their reps "Excellent" at cold calling, and 39% of sellers admit they fear picking up the phone. Buyer resistance has grown too: in 2018 most reps needed one to five touches to land a meeting; in 2026 it typically takes six to ten, and the share needing eleven or more has nearly doubled. It's a direct, current counterpoint to the assumption AI-generated outreach has replaced the phone call as the best way to land a meeting — the data says the opposite, even as buyers grow harder to reach.

Sales angle
Directly relevant to how SDRs prospect NetSuite's own target accounts — phone-based outreach still outperforms AI-written emails for landing meetings, but only when reps push through the growing number of touches now required.
SDR takeaway
Use AI tools to research and personalize before a call, not to replace it — and expect to need more attempts (typically 6-10, sometimes 11+) than reps needed a few years ago before a prospect picks up or replies.
