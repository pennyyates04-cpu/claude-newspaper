Oracle folds "Ask Oracle" AI into NetSuite ahead of SuiteWorld 2026

Oracle has rolled its "Ask Oracle" conversational AI assistant — first introduced with the NetSuite Next redesign — into the mainline NetSuite experience via the 2026.2 release. It lets users ask questions about their data, generate reports, navigate records, and complete tasks in natural language instead of clicking through menus. Oracle's flagship NetSuite conference, SuiteWorld 2026, runs October 25–28 in Las Vegas (plus a virtual "On Air" track), where NetSuite Next is expected to be a centerpiece. This is Oracle pushing generative AI directly into day-to-day ERP workflows for existing customers, not just as a future-release demo — a pattern every major ERP vendor is racing to match.

Oracle's data-center force-majeure notice rattles the stock

Oracle sent a force-majeure notice tied to its "Project Jupiter" data-center campus in New Mexico, citing potential delays in securing power, and is seeking to delay payment obligations if the project doesn't come online as planned by 2028. Shares fell roughly 3% on the news and have stayed under pressure, with Oracle stock struggling to hold above $140 in early October as investors reassess the cost of its aggressive AI data-center buildout. Oracle has bet heavily on AI infrastructure (OCI) to fund growth across its whole portfolio, including NetSuite. Execution risk and financing strain on that bet are now showing up directly in the stock price.

Sales angle
Prospects watching the "is Oracle's AI strategy real or overextended" narrative may ask about it directly — reps should be ready to separate OCI's data-center financing story from NetSuite's own product and AI roadmap.
SDR takeaway
"Force majeure" here is a contract clause Oracle invoked to protect itself from cost overruns if the data center is delayed — not a sign NetSuite itself is in trouble.

Workday cuts ~500 jobs in its second 2026 restructuring

Workday disclosed in a September 29 SEC filing that it will cut approximately 2.5% of its global workforce (about 500 roles), concentrated in its Product and Technology teams, alongside reductions in leased office space. The company expects $65–80 million in restructuring charges and says it will keep hiring in select strategic areas through fiscal 2027. It's Workday's second layoff round of the year, following roughly 400 cuts in February. A direct NetSuite competitor in HCM/ERP is trimming product and engineering headcount twice in one year even as its stock has rallied sharply — a sign of margin discipline pressure across enterprise software, not just struggling companies.

Sales angle

Gartner: only 17% of organizations have deployed AI agents — governance is lagging

Gong: 95% of AI sales deployments miss their expected payoff

Gong's 2026 State of Revenue AI report — based on a survey of 3,048 global revenue leaders and an analysis of 7.1 million sales opportunities — found 87% of US revenue organizations now use AI (with another 9% planning to within 12 months), and AI-driven sales teams generate 77% more revenue per rep and are 65% more likely to grow win rates. But the report also found 95% of AI deployments fall short of their expected commercial impact, with top performers 2–4x more likely to have deployed AI for strategic use cases rather than basic automation.

Sales angle
If a prospect says they "already have AI tools," ask what they're actually using them for — basic automation versus strategic use is the real dividing line, and it's a natural opening for NetSuite's own AI-driven forecasting and reporting.
SDR takeaway
"We use AI" from a prospect doesn't mean much on its own — the 95%-fall-short stat is a good way to probe deeper into what's actually working for them.
