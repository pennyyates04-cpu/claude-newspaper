Mistral AI Raises €3 Billion ($3.5B) Series D Led by Samsung, Europe's Largest-Ever Tech Funding Round

French AI lab Mistral AI announced on September 8 a €3 billion ($3.5 billion) Series D round at a post-money valuation above €21 billion. Samsung Electronics led the round, with the EQT-managed Scaleup Europe Fund and existing investor PSG Equity co-leading. New backers include Advent, BlackRock-managed funds, and the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg; existing investors a16z, ASML, and NVIDIA also participated. Mistral calls it the largest equity round ever completed by a European tech company, and says the money will fund compute, research, and international expansion. The company counts more than 125 enterprise customers, including Airbus and HSBC. It's a concrete signal that non-U.S. "sovereign AI" investment is accelerating fast, with a major hardware player betting directly on a foundation-model company rather than just chips.

Oracle Invokes Force Majeure on $18 Billion "Project Jupiter" Data Center Tied to Its OpenAI Deal

Oracle sent a force majeure notice to Stack Infrastructure, a Blue Owl Capital unit developing its 1,400-acre "Project Jupiter" data center campus in New Mexico — first reported September 24 and since confirmed across multiple outlets. The notice cites delays securing power, a responsibility that falls to Oracle under the lease, after state officials rejected a natural-gas pipeline extension to the site in July. Oracle isn't exiting the project; invoking force majeure lets it keep paying lower "development-stage" rent for longer if the 2028 completion target is missed. Oracle says the project "remains on our planned schedule"; Blue Owl says its own financial commitments are unchanged. The $18 billion campus is part of Oracle's broader infrastructure buildout tied to its deal with OpenAI. It's a concrete example of how hard — and expensive — it's becoming to secure power for AI data centers, even for a company as large as Oracle.

Sales angle
If a prospect is evaluating Oracle Cloud Infrastructure or watching Oracle's broader AI ambitions, this is a useful, concrete data point that infrastructure delivery timelines in AI aren't guaranteed — worth probing how any vendor's AI roadmap commitments are backed operationally.
SDR takeaway
"Force majeure" is a contract clause letting a party delay obligations due to events outside its control; here Oracle is using it defensively to buy time on lease payments, not to exit the deal.
Conversation starter
"Oracle's run into real power-delivery delays on one of its big AI data center projects — has power or infrastructure availability come up as a constraint in any of your own company's AI plans?"

SAP Pushes "Autonomous Enterprise" Toward General Availability With Joule Work at SAP Connect 2026

At its SAP Connect keynote in Las Vegas on October 6, SAP introduced Joule Work, a unified AI workspace bringing together conversations, content, and AI agents across SAP and non-SAP systems within each employee's existing permissions. SAP says the rollout continues through October, and that its own 110,000 employees already use it internally, reporting more than 20% productivity gains in HR, finance, and procurement. The announcement expands SAP's "Autonomous Suite" of AI agents across finance, spend management, HR, supply chain, and customer experience, and adds an agent-to-agent gateway so outside AI systems can call Joule, and vice versa, through SAP's AI Agent Hub. SAP is NetSuite's largest direct ERP competitor, and this is its clearest move yet from scattered AI features toward a single, agent-driven work layer across the whole suite.

Sales angle

Gartner: 69% of B2B Buyers Still Turn to Sales Reps to Validate AI-Generated Research

A Gartner survey released May 20 found that 69% of B2B buyers who use AI during a purchase process turn to a human sales rep to validate the AI-generated insights before deciding, and that buyers now use an average of seven information sources during a typical purchase. The finding follows an earlier Gartner survey in March that found 67% of B2B buyers say they prefer a "rep-free" buying experience in principle, even though 45% reported using AI tools somewhere in a recent purchase. It resolves an apparent contradiction in B2B buying behavior — buyers say they want to self-serve and avoid reps, but the data shows they still need and use reps as a trust-check on AI output, especially before large purchases.

Sales angle
A prospect who's already done extensive AI-assisted research before the first call isn't a bad lead — Gartner's data says that's now the norm, and the SDR's real job has shifted toward confirming and correcting what the AI already told them.
SDR takeaway
Don't assume a well-informed prospect doesn't need you; "validation" — catching gaps, context, or mistakes in AI-generated vendor comparisons — is becoming a core part of what a sales rep provides.

Liferay Study: 54% of Companies Are Running AI Agents, But Only 25% Measure Their Impact

A Liferay-commissioned survey of 500 U.S. professionals involved in AI decision-making, published August 12, found that 54% of companies are already running AI agents in production or piloting them — but only 25% measure that impact with clear KPIs, and only 24% have a company-wide AI usage policy. Adoption varies sharply by industry, from 72% in technology down to 24% in education. It's a current data point behind a pattern many prospects are quietly living through — rapid AI agent rollout outpacing the governance and measurement needed to prove it's actually working.

Sales angle
A prospect that's deployed AI agents but can't show ROI from them is a real opportunity — frame NetSuite's AI capabilities around measurable financial and operational outcomes, not just "we have agents too."
SDR takeaway
"KPIs" (key performance indicators) are the specific, agreed-upon metrics used to judge whether a project succeeded — without them, a company can't actually prove an AI investment paid off, however it feels.
