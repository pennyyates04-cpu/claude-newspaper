ServiceNow launches "AI Workflow Factory" to close the gap between piloting AI agents and running them in production

ServiceNow launched AI Workflow Factory on October 6 at its World Forum event in Mumbai. The product links Process Mining, a new "Autonomous Engineer" agent, App Engine, AI Control Tower, and "Action Fabric" into one loop: find a broken workflow, have AI redesign and build a fix, then keep it governed. The core platform is globally available now; Autonomous Engineer is in early access. Accenture and Infosys are launch partners, with Infosys integrating it into its own Topaz and Cobalt AI platforms. Action Fabric extends ServiceNow's governance layer to third-party AI agents too, not just its own. ServiceNow is explicitly targeting the "AI execution gap" — companies that have piloted AI agents but can't yet run them safely at scale — the same problem Oracle, SAP, Salesforce, and Workday are all racing to solve right now.

Sales angle
ServiceNow is positioning itself as a governance layer sitting atop other vendors' AI agents — worth knowing if a prospect runs ServiceNow for IT workflows and asks how NetSuite's AI features would coexist with it.
SDR takeaway
"AI execution gap" is useful shorthand for the distance between piloting an AI agent and trusting it to run unsupervised in production — a concern that comes up constantly in enterprise AI talks.

Open-source AI agent startup Nous Research raises $90M, launches enterprise product "Hermes for Businesses"

Nous Research, maker of the open-source Hermes AI agent, raised a $90 million round led by Robot Ventures at a $1.5 billion valuation, with backers including Nvidia, Union Square Ventures, Menlo Ventures, and Samsung, per reports dated October 7 citing the Wall Street Journal. The round funds "Hermes for Businesses," which lets companies pick which underlying AI model powers their agents by cost or goal while keeping data private. It's a concrete, fast-growing example of "bring-your-own-model" enterprise AI agents — where a business picks its AI model rather than being locked into one vendor's — gaining real commercial traction.

Manufacturing kept growing in September, but input prices spiked sharply

The ISM's September 2026 Manufacturing PMI, released October 1, came in at 54.5% — just 0.1 point below August — marking a ninth straight month of sector expansion. Manufacturing is still growing and even adding orders and jobs, but the sharp jump in input costs is a direct margin squeeze — exactly the kind of pressure that pushes manufacturers and distributors to look harder at cost visibility and inventory tools.
