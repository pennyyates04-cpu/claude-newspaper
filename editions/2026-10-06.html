AWS signs $1B+ chip-design deal aimed partly at speeding up SAP, Salesforce, and Workday

Amazon Web Services signed a multiyear chip-design licensing deal worth more than $1 billion with Synopsys, naming AWS the lead customer for Synopsys's expanding "application-optimized" silicon IP business — chip blueprints tailored to specific workloads rather than generic components. The deal also expands AWS's use of Synopsys's electronic design automation (EDA) software, and the two companies plan to collaborate on applying AI across chip-to-system engineering. A stated goal of the new custom silicon is to boost performance for major enterprise apps running on AWS — SAP, Salesforce, and Workday were specifically named. Faster, cheaper infrastructure under the hood lets those vendors improve price-performance without touching their own application code.

Sales angle
Cloud infrastructure strategy is usually invisible to buyers, but "AI-optimized infrastructure" is becoming a selling point in enterprise RFPs. Prospects evaluating ERP or CRM systems may start asking what cloud and chip strategy underpins a vendor's performance claims.
SDR takeaway
EDA (electronic design automation) is the software used to design computer chips. This deal shows the AI infrastructure race moving down to the hardware level — hyperscalers are now building custom silicon to run everyone else's software faster.

Schneider Electric to acquire PTC for $22.6 billion

Schneider Electric signed a definitive agreement to acquire PTC Inc. — maker of industrial design and product lifecycle management (PLM) software including Creo and Windchill — for $22.6 billion in cash, or $205 per share, a roughly 42% premium to PTC's prior closing price. It's the largest acquisition in Schneider's history. The deal is expected to close by Q3 2027, pending PTC shareholder approval and regulatory clearance. Schneider, a major industrial energy-management and automation company, is betting that owning PTC's design and engineering software lets it sell a complete "digital thread" running from product design through the factory floor to energy operations — a direct play into the industrial AI and smart-manufacturing push.

Sales angle
Any manufacturing or industrial prospect using PTC tools is about to go through a vendor-ownership change — a classic moment when companies reassess their broader software stack, including ERP and financial systems that need to integrate with design and manufacturing data.
SDR takeaway
PLM (product lifecycle management) software manages a product's data from initial design through manufacturing and service. This deal shows industrial giants buying software companies outright rather than just partnering with them — consolidation in industrial tech is accelerating.
Conversation starter

NetSuite 2026.2 rolls out "Ask Oracle" and "NetSuite Next"

Oracle is rolling out NetSuite 2026.2, headlined by "NetSuite Next," a redesigned interface, and "Ask Oracle," a conversational AI assistant built directly into NetSuite that lets users ask business questions in plain language instead of building a saved search. The release also adds "AI Canvas," an interactive workspace combining KPI scorecards, charts, and AI-generated summaries on one page. Ask Oracle and NetSuite Next launched first for US and Canada accounts, with more countries planned. This moves AI from a bolt-on feature into the core of how people use NetSuite every day, following Oracle management's claim of deploying more than 100 AI agents across the platform during fiscal 2026.

Sales angle
Prospects comparing ERP systems increasingly ask about embedded AI rather than bolt-on AI add-ons. Ask Oracle and AI Canvas give concrete, demoable answers to "how does your AI actually work inside the system" instead of a marketing slide.
SDR takeaway
A "saved search" is NetSuite's traditional tool for building custom reports and queries, which typically takes some training to use well. Ask Oracle is meant to replace that with natural-language questions — a real usability shift for finance and operations teams.

OpenAI introduces "Dots," persistent AI agents for business tasks

At its September 29, 2026 DevDay, OpenAI introduced "Dots" — persistent AI agents, powered by GPT-6 Astra, that keep working toward a stated goal across connected apps after a user steps away, rather than needing a new prompt for every step. OpenAI says specialized Dots can be given their own organizational identity, credentials, and business responsibilities, with early pilots in procurement, invoice processing, customer support, and contracting. Dots are reachable through ChatGPT, Slack, and Microsoft Teams, rolling out first to Pro and Business Premium users, with Enterprise availability controlled by workspace admins. This pushes general-purpose AI further into territory ERP and CRM vendors already compete in — ongoing, autonomous task execution tied to real business systems, rather than one-off chat answers.

Sales angle
As AI agents with their own credentials start handling procurement and invoicing, prospects will increasingly ask how a given platform's AI fits (or conflicts) with tools like Dots — questions about data access, security, and "who owns this workflow" become live issues in a sales conversation.
SDR takeaway
This is "agentic AI" in practice — AI that acts on tasks over time, not just answers questions. A Dot can carry out a multi-step task over hours or days without a human prompting each individual step, a meaningful jump from a standard chatbot.
Conversation starter

Dynatrace closes $915M acquisition of AI-observability startup Arize

Dynatrace completed its $915 million acquisition of Arize, an AI observability and evaluation platform, on October 1, 2026. The deal combines Arize's AI-native tracing and evaluation tools — built for monitoring models and agents — with Dynatrace's existing observability across applications, infrastructure, and business processes. Arize will keep supporting its open-source Phoenix platform and its enterprise AX platform while its capabilities are integrated into Dynatrace over time. As companies roll out more AI agents, someone has to monitor whether those agents are working correctly, hallucinating, or going off-script — this deal signals that "AI agent observability" is becoming its own must-have software category.

Sales angle
Any company deploying multiple AI agents across its software stack — including NetSuite customers using its growing roster of embedded AI agents — will eventually need a way to monitor what those agents are actually doing, a new line item in the IT budget.
SDR takeaway
"Observability" refers to monitoring tools that show what's happening inside software systems in real time — performance, errors, usage. "AI observability" extends that specifically to tracking model and agent behavior, a new and fast-growing niche.

HubSpot ships "Deal Progression" to recommend a rep's next move

The week of October 5, 2026, HubSpot shipped Deal Progression: Next Best Actions and Deal Plans inside Sales Hub. The feature reads the emails, calls, and meetings tied to a deal, then recommends next actions, drafts follow-up emails, and proposes CRM updates for a rep to approve — instead of requiring reps to dig through transcripts and notes themselves. HubSpot says customers using Deal Progression close 2.2x more deals on average. This is a shipped, concrete example of AI agents doing sales-rep-adjacent work, not just assisting — useful both as a competitive data point against NetSuite's and Salesforce's own AI roadmaps, and as a preview of where "AI SDR" tooling is heading industry-wide.

Sales angle
Prospects already using or evaluating HubSpot will have specific functionality to compare against NetSuite's AI capabilities — knowing the details here helps position NetSuite credibly rather than generically when AI comes up.
SDR takeaway
"Next best action" tools are becoming standard in modern sales software — AI reads deal activity and tells a rep what to do next instead of the rep piecing it together manually. This affects how SDR work itself will change going forward.
