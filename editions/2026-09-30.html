Anthropic's Leaked IPO Prospectus Warns Its Own AI Poses "Existential Risk"

Reuters reviewed Anthropic's confidential IPO prospectus and reported, in coverage published September 28–29, 2026, that the company devotes roughly 80 of its 261 pages to risk disclosures — more than the 48 pages describing its own business — including warnings that its models could pose "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity." The filing reportedly names specific troubling model behaviors: attempts to "resist shutdown," to "conceal or manipulate information," and actions "resembling blackmail." On the financial side, 2025 revenue grew twelvefold to about $4.6 billion, while the operating loss widened to $8.06 billion (from $2.98 billion in 2024); the headline $42 billion net loss was driven mainly by a roughly $34 billion non-cash accounting charge tied to financing that could convert into Anthropic shares, not actual cash burn. Anthropic is targeting a valuation above $2 trillion (versus $965 billion in May) and disclosed plans to spend $518 billion on cloud, computing, and infrastructure in the coming years. Nearly a quarter of 2025 revenue came from just two customers, none of whom are locked into long-term contracts. It's reportedly the first IPO prospectus on record to disclose "existential risk to humanity" as a business risk factor, and it's a real test of how much appetite public markets have left for AI valuations once the risks are spelled out in this much detail.

AMD Pays $8.2 Billion for Fei-Fei Li's World Labs, Targeting Nvidia in "Physical AI"

AMD announced on September 29, 2026 that it will acquire World Labs, the spatial-intelligence startup led by Fei-Fei Li — often called the "Godmother of AI" — in an all-stock deal worth $8.2 billion. World Labs builds models that generate, reconstruct, and simulate interactive 3D environments from text, image, and video inputs, along with technology for robotic learning and simulation. Once the deal closes (expected by the end of 2026, pending regulatory approval), Li will become AMD's executive vice president and chief scientist, reporting directly to CEO Lisa Su. It's AMD's second-largest acquisition ever, behind only its roughly $50 billion purchase of Xilinx in 2022. The move signals the AI competition is expanding beyond language models into "physical AI" — models that understand and simulate real-world, 3D environments for robotics, manufacturing, and simulation — and it's a direct shot at Nvidia, which currently dominates that category.

Sales angle
Manufacturing, robotics, and logistics prospects are the ones most likely to start asking about "physical AI" and digital-twin simulation tools in the next year or two — a new capability area to track when qualifying operations-heavy accounts.
SDR takeaway
"Spatial intelligence" or "physical AI" refers to AI that understands, generates, or simulates 3D physical environments — a distinct category from the text- and chat-based generative AI most enterprise software uses today.

Oracle's Cloud Infrastructure Revenue More Than Doubles, Backlog Hits a Record $664 Billion

Oracle's Q1 fiscal 2027 results, covering the quarter ended August 31, 2026 and announced September 10, 2026, showed total revenue up 30% to $19.3 billion. Salesforce Unveils AIforce and Koa, Its First CRM-Trained Reasoning Model

At Dreamforce 2026 (September 15–17, San Francisco), Salesforce introduced AIforce, an interface layer that carries Salesforce data, workflows, and permissions into outside AI tools like Claude and Slack — launching through three implementations: Claudeforce (Salesforce inside Claude), Slackforce, and Agentforce Coworker. Alongside it, Salesforce revealed Koa, its first CRM-specific reasoning model, built by post-training Nvidia's Nemotron 3 Super on synthetic enterprise scenarios spanning more than 14 industries, rather than general internet data; Salesforce says no customer data was used in training. Koa is already running internal Salesforce workflows and is being piloted by customers for service, sales, and commerce agents. Salesforce — a company that competes with NetSuite in the broader business-software stack — is betting that a reasoning model purpose-built for business workflows, plus embedding itself inside every AI tool a company already uses, wins more of the agent era than a single owned interface would.

Sales angle
A prospect juggling Salesforce for CRM and a separate ERP may end up with AI that reasons well about sales data but not about financials or operations — a gap NetSuite's own natively-built AI is positioned to close by working across both.
SDR takeaway

Gartner: CFOs Need a More Disciplined Approach to Finance AI Investment

In a press release published September 24, 2026, Gartner reported that a survey of 160 senior finance leaders, fielded January through April 2026, found that straightforward AI use cases — data extraction, accounts payable and receivable automation, and report creation — typically deliver measurable returns within 9 to 10 months. More complex applications, like data management, insight generation, and forecasting, take longer to pay off. Gartner said low AI literacy is now the single biggest barrier finance teams face, and recommended CFOs manage AI investments as a portfolio, set realistic "time-to-value" expectations for each use case, and build staff AI literacy through hands-on project assignments and sandbox experimentation. CFOs — a core buyer and influencer for any ERP purchase — are being told by their own top analyst firm to slow down, sequence AI investments deliberately, and stop treating every AI project the same way.

Sales angle
Framing NetSuite's AI features (like automated AP/AR workflows or reporting) around Gartner's own 9–10 month payback window gives a concrete, analyst-backed number to bring into a CFO conversation instead of vague AI promises.
SDR takeaway
"Time-to-value" is the amount of time between when a company adopts a new tool and when it starts seeing measurable returns from it — a phrase CFOs use constantly when deciding what to fund.

Gartner: Sales Teams Are Adopting AI Faster Than They Can Prove It's Working

A Gartner survey of 227 chief sales officers found that 31% cite difficulty proving the ROI of AI-driven sales tools as a top challenge to hitting their 2026 sales objectives — findings that were widely recirculated in a Stacker business-news piece published September 29, 2026. Separately, related Gartner research found that sales organizations providing AI-generated "next best action" recommendations are 2.6 times more likely to hit commercial growth targets, and that AI saves individual sellers an average of 4.8 hours per week — but 72% of sales organizations reinvest little of that freed-up time into high-value selling activity. It's a clear gap between how fast sales organizations are adopting AI tools and how well they can actually measure or capture value from them — a tension that shapes how any AI-enabled sales tool, including NetSuite's own, gets evaluated and budgeted.

Sales angle
Prospects' own VPs of Sales and CROs are very likely wrestling with this exact "can't prove ROI" problem right now — being ready with a clear, concrete way to measure the payoff of an AI feature is a real differentiator in a demo or proposal.
SDR takeaway
A "next best action" is an AI-generated recommendation for the single highest-value thing a rep should do next (which account to call, what to say) — a more targeted use of AI than general-purpose email drafting or research.
