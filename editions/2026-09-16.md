## Revolut Confirms Breach After Attackers Posed as a Government Agency

**What happened:** Fintech Revolut confirmed on Saturday, September 12, 2026, that it had disclosed customer personal data to attackers who impersonated a government agency through a spoofed but domain-authenticated email requesting the information. Reporting put the confirmed impact at nearly 700 clients, with exposed data including birth dates, addresses, phone numbers, and copies of passports or driver's licenses; the attacker separately claimed a broader six-month operation that included compromising Italian law-enforcement systems to file fraudulent data requests. **Why it matters:** One of the largest fintechs was breached not through a hacked system but by someone convincingly posing as a legitimate government requester — a reminder that identity-verification workflows, not just software security, are where financial-services companies are exposed.

**Sales angle:** For prospects in financial services, this is a real, current example to reference when discussing how their finance and compliance workflows verify who's actually requesting sensitive data.
