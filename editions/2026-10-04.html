Micron's AI-driven memory boom keeps beating estimates

Micron reported fiscal Q4 2026 revenue of $54.23 billion, above the $51.07 billion analysts expected, with adjusted earnings of $33.42 per share versus a $31.61 estimate. Data center revenue roughly quadrupled year-over-year, DRAM revenue topped $100 billion for the fiscal year, and Micron guided next quarter to about $61.5 billion in revenue and $38.15 in adjusted EPS — both above Street expectations. Full fiscal-year 2026 revenue hit a record $133.2 billion, up 256% from the prior year. Micron makes the memory chips that feed AI data centers, so its results are a real-time read on how much the industry is actually spending to build out AI infrastructure — and demand is still outrunning supply.

Salesforce debuts "Koa," its first CRM reasoning model, built with NVIDIA

At Dreamforce 2026, Salesforce introduced Koa, described as its first CRM reasoning model for Agentforce, built on NVIDIA's Nemotron technology for agent reasoning, tool use, and multi-step decision-making. Salesforce says Koa trains entirely on synthetic data rather than real customer data, and the company owns and runs the model's weights on its own infrastructure. Koa is currently in pilot with customers including Formula 1 and UChicago Medicine, with general availability expected in winter 2026. Salesforce building and owning its own reasoning model — rather than solely relying on third-party LLMs — is a significant competitive move in the race to make AI agents handle complex, multi-step CRM workflows instead of just answering simple questions.

Sales angle
