Nvidia Lines Up Over $500 Billion in Outside Financing for AI Infrastructure

Nvidia announced preliminary agreements with six major financial institutions — Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR — to establish independent financing platforms aimed at mobilizing more than $500 billion in third-party capital for AI data-center and power buildout. Rather than Nvidia financing the projects itself, the structure uses Nvidia compute hardware as collateral for debt raised through private offerings and special-purpose entities, keeping the spending off Nvidia's own balance sheet. The AI buildout has gotten so large that even the industry's most valuable company doesn't want to carry all of it on its own books. Routing hundreds of billions through Wall Street signals both how much capital this next phase requires and how much financial engineering now underpins the AI boom.

Sales angle
This is the financial plumbing behind the AI capacity that prospects' own cloud and AI vendors run on — when a prospect complains about rising cloud or GPU-access costs, this financing wave (and the debt behind it) is part of why.
SDR takeaway
"Off-balance-sheet financing" — funding an asset through a separate entity so the debt doesn't show up on the parent company's own balance sheet — is a term increasingly used to describe how the AI infrastructure race is actually being paid for; useful vocabulary for finance-savvy prospects.
Conversation starter

Oracle NetSuite Begins Rolling Out "NetSuite Next," Its AI-First Redesign

As part of the NetSuite 2026.2 release, Oracle NetSuite started the North America rollout of NetSuite Next — a redesigned version of NetSuite with AI built into its core rather than bolted on. The centerpiece is Ask Oracle, a conversational assistant that lets users ask plain-language questions to search records, generate reports, and complete tasks instead of building saved searches or navigating menus. The release also adds AI Canvas, a workspace that pulls KPI scorecards, charts, and AI-generated summaries onto one page, plus a more automated bank-reconciliation workflow. The rollout is happening in phases through October and isn't automatic on upgrade. This is Oracle's most direct answer yet to the "AI-native ERP" pitch competitors have been making — natural-language interaction and automated reconciliation attack two of the most common complaints about legacy ERP: it's hard to find information, and closing the books is manual and slow.

Sales angle
Prospects evaluating NetSuite against a competitor now have a tangible, near-term feature set to compare — not just a roadmap promise — which can shorten the "does it actually do X" phase of a deal.
SDR takeaway
When a prospect brings up "AI in NetSuite," be ready to walk through concrete examples — Ask Oracle for natural-language reporting, AI Canvas for a single-page KPI view, and automated reconciliation — rather than speaking about "AI" abstractly.
Conversation starter

Fed's September Rate Call Flips From Likely Cut to Near-Coin-Flip on a Hike

Just a week after a blowout August jobs report fueled expectations of a near-term rate cut, the outlook reversed. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole speech on August 28 — emphasizing his commitment to fighting inflation — pushed fed-funds-futures traders' odds of a quarter-point hike at the September 16 meeting from roughly 44% to 56–60% within days, per CME's FedWatch tool, while prediction markets Kalshi and Polymarket both moved to roughly a coin flip. A potential hike, rather than a cut, would be a significant reversal for financial markets and corporate borrowing costs, and it shows how much a single Fed leadership change and one speech can move rate expectations in days.

Sales angle

SDR takeaway
"Fed funds rate" is the short-term interest rate the Federal Reserve sets, which influences borrowing costs across the economy, from corporate credit lines to mortgages; a hike makes borrowing more expensive, a cut makes it cheaper.

Uber Cuts 10% of Its Workforce in Its Biggest Layoffs Since the Pandemic

Uber announced it is cutting about 3,300 jobs — roughly 10% of its global workforce — in what CEO Dara Khosrowshahi described as an effort to remove management layers, shrink redundant teams, and let the company "move simpler and faster." The plan cuts the number of managers by 20% and redirects the savings toward its core ridesharing and delivery businesses and its autonomous-vehicle (robotaxi) push. It's Uber's largest round of layoffs since it cut 14% of staff during the pandemic in 2020. Uber is profitable and growing, so this isn't a distress cut — it's a bet that a flatter, smaller organization can move faster on its next big initiative (autonomy) than a larger one, a pattern worth watching at other large tech companies.

Sales angle
Companies going through this kind of organizational flattening often re-evaluate their software stack at the same time, consolidating tools and renegotiating vendor contracts as team structures change — worth watching for in similarly sized accounts.
SDR takeaway
"Delayering" — removing layers of middle management to speed up decisions — is the term for what Uber is doing; it's becoming a common efficiency move across large tech companies, not just a cost-cutting tactic.

New G2 Research: AI Is Reshaping How B2B Software Deals Are Won and Lost

G2's 2026 Buyer Behavior Report, based on a survey of over 1,000 B2B software buyers, found 51% now start vendor research in an AI chatbot rather than a search engine or review site, and 82% have sourced a software recommendation from one in the past two years. But buyers remain skeptical of what those tools tell them: 94% fact-check AI-generated answers at least some of the time, and demos, trials, and peer reviews still drive the final decision. Internal resistance is also growing — the share of buyers citing internal pushback nearly doubled year over year (16% to 29%), and 49% said a CFO vetoed an already-approved purchase in the last 12 months. AI has compressed the early "discovery" stage of B2B buying into a single prompt, but it hasn't shortened the deal — it's shifted the hard part to validation and internal approval, which is exactly where a human seller adds the most value.

Sales angle
If a prospect's shortlist increasingly comes from an AI chatbot, showing up well in AI-generated answers (accurate product info, strong reviews, clear differentiation) matters as much as traditional SEO or outbound now.
SDR takeaway
Don't assume a prospect who mentions "I asked ChatGPT about ERP options" has done less homework than one who read analyst reports — they've likely done more, faster, and will expect you to go deeper, not repeat what the AI already told them.
Conversation starter
"A lot of buyers are starting their research with AI tools these days — did you look into any ERP options that way before we connected?"

Zoho Launches Catalyst 3.0, Betting on AI Coding Agents Over Traditional Developer Tools

Zoho launched Catalyst 3.0, an update to its full-stack cloud development platform aimed at developers who build software using AI coding assistants. It adds "Agent Skills," Model Context Protocol (MCP) support, and direct integrations with coding agents including Anthropic's Claude Code and OpenAI's Codex, letting a developer go from an AI-generated prototype to production-grade hosting, databases, and functions without leaving their coding environment. Zoho — a direct NetSuite competitor in accounting, CRM, and business software — is positioning its platform around AI-agent-driven development specifically, rather than treating AI coding tools as a bolt-on feature. It's a signal of where mid-market software vendors see the next competitive battleground.

Sales angle

SK Hynix CEO Warns 2027 Will Be the Memory Chip Industry's "Worst Year Ever" for Supply

SK Hynix CEO Kwak Noh-jung told Reuters that global memory chip supply — DRAM and the high-bandwidth memory (HBM) used in AI chips — will fall further short of demand in 2027 than at any point in the industry's history, and the shortage will likely persist beyond 2030 despite aggressive new factory investment. Samsung has issued similar warnings. The shortage is driven almost entirely by AI demand, with DRAM contract prices already up 90–95% quarter-over-quarter earlier in 2026. Memory chips go into nearly every piece of business hardware — servers, laptops, phones — not just AI systems, so this shortage is on track to raise IT hardware costs broadly across the economy for at least the next two years.

Sales angle
Rising hardware costs make the "why buy new servers when you can run this in the cloud" argument more compelling for prospects still weighing on-premise versus cloud ERP.
SDR takeaway
"HBM" (high-bandwidth memory) is the specialized, expensive memory chip type AI processors need; it's a big part of why AI compute is expensive and in short supply right now.
