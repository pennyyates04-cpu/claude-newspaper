Oracle Posts Blowout Q1 Results — $664 Billion Backlog, NetSuite Next Hits 10,000 Customers

Oracle reported fiscal Q1 FY2027 results on September 10, 2026: revenue of $19.35 billion, up 30% year-over-year and ahead of the $19.14 billion analysts expected, with adjusted earnings per share of $1.92, also beating estimates. Total cloud revenue rose 62% to $11.61 billion, and cloud infrastructure (OCI) revenue alone surged 121% to $7.39 billion. Remaining Performance Obligations — Oracle's contracted backlog — hit $664 billion, up $209 billion from a year earlier, after the company booked more than $30 billion in new AI cloud contracts during the quarter. Capital spending jumped to $28.5 billion from $8.5 billion a year ago, pushing free cash flow negative even as operating cash flow stayed positive. Fusion ERP grew 14%, and Oracle disclosed that NetSuite Next — the AI-powered NetSuite experience rolled out in North America starting in July — has been adopted by 10,000 customers. Oracle raised its FY2027 guidance to at least $90 billion in revenue. This is the clearest data point yet on how fast NetSuite Next is actually spreading among customers, and it shows Oracle is willing to run cash flow negative to keep funding the AI buildout that underpins its cloud pitch.

Salesforce Unveils AIforce at Dreamforce, Betting CRM Moves Into Chat and Slack

At Dreamforce 2026 (September 15–17, San Francisco), Salesforce announced AIforce on September 15 — a new interface layer meant to carry Salesforce's data, workflows, and permissions into whatever AI tool an employee already uses, rather than requiring them to log into Salesforce itself. Its first pieces are Claudeforce, an extension of Salesforce's Anthropic partnership that packages 37 prebuilt sales skills into an MCP server inside Claude, piloted by Deloitte, GitLab, and Legora and now in beta; and Slackforce, which surfaces live Salesforce context inside Slack. Salesforce also introduced Koa, its first CRM-specific AI reasoning model, built on Nvidia's Nemotron, and expanded partnerships with Google Cloud and AWS. NetSuite's biggest CRM-adjacent competitor is betting that customers will manage their CRM data from inside AI chat tools rather than a dedicated app — a framing worth understanding since prospects on Salesforce will likely raise it.

Revolut Confirms Breach After Attackers Posed as a Government Agency

Fintech Revolut confirmed on Saturday, September 12, 2026, that it had disclosed customer personal data to attackers who impersonated a government agency through a spoofed but domain-authenticated email requesting the information. Reporting put the confirmed impact at nearly 700 clients, with exposed data including birth dates, addresses, phone numbers, and copies of passports or driver's licenses; the attacker separately claimed a broader six-month operation that included compromising Italian law-enforcement systems to file fraudulent data requests. Revolut described it as "a sophisticated external impersonation scam." One of the largest fintechs was breached not through a hacked system but by someone convincingly posing as a legitimate government requester — a reminder that identity-verification workflows, not just software security, are where financial-services companies are exposed.

Sales angle
For prospects in financial services, this is a real, current example to reference when discussing how their finance and compliance workflows verify who's actually requesting sensitive data.
SDR takeaway
"Social engineering" — tricking a person rather than breaking a system — is now behind some of the biggest breaches at well-funded, security-mature companies; worth recognizing as distinct from a technical hack.

CISA Flags Actively Exploited Bugs in JFrog Artifactory, ConnectWise ScreenConnect, and MikroTik Routers

On September 12, 2026, the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) added five vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog: two JFrog Artifactory flaws (CVE-2026-42016 and CVE-2026-42018) that attackers chained with another bug to seize admin control of self-hosted servers between August 15 and September 8; a ConnectWise ScreenConnect flaw (CVE-2026-84869, rated a near-maximum 9.9 severity) letting an attacker transfer and run files over an active remote-support session without authorization; and two MikroTik RouterOS flaws. Federal agencies must patch the ConnectWise flaw by September 14 and the Artifactory bugs by September 25. ConnectWise ScreenConnect is widely used by the managed service providers and IT support firms that many mid-market companies — including NetSuite prospects — rely on, so a near-maximum-severity, actively exploited flaw there is a live operational risk, not just background security news.
