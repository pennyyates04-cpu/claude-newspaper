## Salesforce Gives Agentforce Seven Named Agents — Two of Them Aimed Straight at the SDR Job

**What happened:** On September 11, Salesforce rolled out a new portfolio of seven named, "job-ready" Agentforce AI agents, each built for a specific function: Casey (customer service), Paige (IT/HR), Carter (shopping/commerce), Marshall (back-office operations), and — most relevant here — Hunter and Piper, both aimed at sales pipeline work. Hunter researches accounts and runs outbound outreach over weeks and months using a new "long-horizon runtime" that lets it pursue a goal across many sessions instead of one chat; it's still in pilot until November. Piper works inbound leads across web and email to qualify and convert them into pipeline. Salesforce also introduced the Trusted Enterprise AI Harness, a governance layer for companies running agents from multiple vendors, and closed its $3.6 billion acquisition of customer-experience AI company Fin the same week.
**Why it matters:** This is Salesforce explicitly building AI agents to do outbound prospecting and inbound qualification — the core of an SDR's job — not just support functions. **SDR takeaway:** A "long-horizon runtime" is an AI agent architecture that keeps working toward a goal over days or weeks rather than resetting after each conversation — a term worth recognizing as more vendors build it.

## Nvidia to Buy Hugging Face for $12.93 Billion, Extending Its Reach From Chips to AI Software

**What happened:** Nvidia confirmed on September 3 it will acquire Hugging Face, the platform more than 18 million developers use to share and deploy AI models, for $12.93 billion. CEO Jensen Huang said Nvidia will invest in scaling Hugging Face's infrastructure and pledged it will "remain an open platform for the entire AI ecosystem," with no requirement to use Nvidia hardware. The deal still needs regulatory approval.
**Why it matters:** Hugging Face is essentially the default distribution hub for open AI models — more than 200,000 companies use it to find and deploy them. Owning it puts Nvidia in a dominant position in both the hardware and software layers of the AI stack it already leads.

**Sales angle:** Any prospect building or deploying AI models in-house likely touches Hugging Face somewhere in their workflow — this deal is a useful, low-pressure way to ask how a customer is thinking about AI infrastructure vendor concentration and lock-in.
**SDR takeaway:** Nvidia moving up the AI "stack" from chips into model distribution and software is part of a broader pattern of infrastructure vendors buying their way into adjacent layers — worth watching for the same pattern at Oracle, Microsoft, and others.
**Conversation starter:** "Nvidia just agreed to buy Hugging Face, the platform a lot of teams use to find AI models — has that kind of vendor consolidation come up as a concern for how your team plans its AI roadmap?"

## Larry Ellison Cancels Plan to Sell $7.5 Billion of Oracle Stock, a Day After Disclosing It

**What happened:** Oracle Chairman and co-founder Larry Ellison canceled a Rule 10b5-1 trading plan to sell as many as 50 million Oracle shares — worth roughly $7.5 billion — on September 12, just one day after the plan's existence was disclosed. The plan had been adopted on June 22, when the shares were worth about $8.75 billion; Oracle's stock has fallen roughly 16% since then. Oracle didn't give a reason for the reversal, and Ellison said he has no other current plans to sell Oracle stock. (A Rule 10b5-1 plan is a pre-scheduled stock-sale arrangement executives use to sell shares without being accused of trading on inside information.)

## HubSpot Becomes the First CRM in OpenAI's New Small-Business Connector Collection

**What happened:** HubSpot announced this month that its ChatGPT connector — which lets users query CRM data and run analysis directly inside ChatGPT using natural language — was added to OpenAI's newly launched small-business collection, making it easier for small-business teams to discover and turn on. The connection is read-only: ChatGPT can analyze HubSpot data but can't edit records, and HubSpot data isn't used to train OpenAI's general models.
**Why it matters:** It's a concrete example of a major CRM competitor embedding itself directly into the tool millions of business users already open every day, rather than asking customers to log into a separate app — a distribution advantage worth tracking.

**Sales angle:** A prospect currently on HubSpot who's excited about "AI in their CRM" is likely reacting to this kind of ChatGPT integration — useful to know so you can compare it directly to how Ask Oracle works inside NetSuite itself, without needing a separate app.
**SDR takeaway:** A "connector" in this context is a sanctioned integration that lets an outside AI tool like ChatGPT securely read data from a business app — expect more vendors to ship these as ChatGPT's business use grows.
**Conversation starter:** "Some CRMs are starting to plug directly into ChatGPT for reporting and analysis — is that the kind of AI feature that's been coming up in what your team's evaluating?"

## Zero-Day Bug Lets Hackers Backdoor Fully Patched Magento and Adobe Commerce Stores

**What happened:** Security researchers disclosed "StyleSmuggler" (CVE-2026-75650), an unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaw in Magento Open Source and Adobe Commerce that attackers were actively exploiting to plant backdoors starting around September 4 — even on stores running the latest patches. Adobe shipped an emergency hotfix on September 7 with its highest priority rating; the U.S. government's cybersecurity agency (CISA) added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 8 and gave federal agencies until September 11 to patch it.
**Why it matters:** Magento and Adobe Commerce power a large share of mid-market e-commerce sites, and "unauthenticated" means no login is required to exploit it — any retail or e-commerce prospect running either platform needs to know this exists and be actively patching, not just relying on their last update.

**Sales angle:** If you're prospecting retail, wholesale distribution, or e-commerce companies, this is a timely, genuinely useful reason to reach out — not as a scare tactic, but as a real operational risk their team may already be dealing with this week.
**SDR takeaway:** A "zero-day" is a vulnerability being actively exploited before, or right as, a fix becomes available — the most urgent category of security bug, and one that shows up in the news often enough to recognize by name.
**Conversation starter:** "There's a pretty serious security bug going around for Magento and Adobe Commerce stores right now — has your team's e-commerce platform been affected by anything like that recently?"
