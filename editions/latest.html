Oracle NetSuite Pushes Toward an "Autonomous Suite" at SuiteWorld 2026

What happened: At SuiteWorld 2026 in Las Vegas on October 26, Oracle NetSuite announced a push toward what it calls an "Autonomous Suite" — AI Workspaces for coordinating people, AI agents, and approvals on complex processes; SuiteSkills and AI Portlets; and industry-specific SuiteAgents for software, product, and construction businesses, all now available to NetSuite Next customers in the US and Canada. A broader agent-building tool, SuiteAgent Studio, along with a SuiteFlow Agent and new Ask Oracle connectors, is planned for a future release. Why it matters: This is Oracle's clearest statement yet that it wants NetSuite's AI to move from answering questions (Ask Oracle) to actually executing multi-step finance and operations work — directly matching where Microsoft, SAP, and Salesforce are all racing to take their own platforms.

Sales angle
A NetSuite prospect comparing ERP vendors is increasingly asking not "does it have AI" but "can the AI actually take action in my system" — this rollout gives concrete, available-now examples to point to rather than roadmap promises.
SDR takeaway
An "agent" in this context is software that can carry out a multi-step task (like reconciling an account) rather than just answering a question — the difference between an agent and a chatbot is whether it can act, not just respond.

ServiceNow Beats on Revenue, Shares Sink ~19% on Soft Bookings Guidance

What happened: ServiceNow reported Q3 2026 revenue of $4.11 billion, up 20.5% year over year and above the high end of its own guidance, but shares fell roughly 19% after the company guided Q4 current remaining performance obligations (cRPO) growth to about 18.5%, down from 21% the prior quarter. Why it matters: A strong quarter that still gets punished shows investors are now judging ERP/CRM-adjacent software vendors less on current revenue and more on whether AI products are actually converting into durable, forward bookings — a bar NetSuite and every competitor will be held to.

Alphabet's AI Capex Scare and Microsoft's Smoother Landing

What happened: Alphabet and Microsoft both reported quarterly results on October 28. Alphabet's revenue reached $127.6 billion (up about 25%, with Google Cloud up over 100%), but capital spending of $51.4 billion pushed free cash flow to negative $9.1 billion, and shares fell roughly 8% after hours even as Alphabet held its 2026 capex guidance of $195–205 billion and signaled 2027 spending would rise further. Microsoft's results the same day — Azure growth of 48% and $92.3 billion in revenue — were received far more positively, with shares rising despite capital spending also roughly doubling year over year. Why it matters: Two of the biggest AI infrastructure spenders posted a similar story — explosive cloud growth paired with enormous capital spending — but got opposite stock reactions, showing investors are now scrutinizing how companies spend on AI, not just how much.

Sales angle
Every enterprise software buyer's IT and finance leadership is watching this same tension play out at the biggest companies in tech — a timely way to ask a prospect how rising cloud or AI infrastructure costs are factoring into their own technology budget conversations.
SDR takeaway
"Capex" (capital expenditure) is money spent on long-term assets like data centers and chips; free cash flow is what's left after capex — when capex grows faster than revenue, free cash flow can go negative even at a profitable, fast-growing company.

Critical, No-Login Oracle E-Business Suite Flaw Already Being Scanned For

What happened: Oracle's October 20, 2026 Critical Patch Update fixed more than 1,200 vulnerabilities across its product line, including CVE-2026-101767, a critical (CVSS 9.8) flaw in E-Business Suite's Application Object Library that can be exploited remotely without any login. By October 27–28, threat-intelligence researchers and BleepingComputer reported attackers were already scanning the internet for systems that hadn't yet applied the fix. Why it matters: A no-credentials-needed flaw in a widely used ERP suite, under active scanning within about a week of disclosure, is exactly the kind of incident that pushes IT and security teams to accelerate patch cycles and scrutinize how quickly their software vendors ship and communicate fixes.

Sales angle
A prospect running Oracle E-Business Suite — a common on-premises predecessor to cloud ERP — may be more open right now to a conversation about the operational burden of patching and securing on-prem systems versus a cloud-managed platform.
SDR takeaway
"Remotely exploitable without authentication" means an attacker doesn't need a valid login to exploit the flaw — it's why bugs like this get the highest severity scores and the fastest attention from security teams.
