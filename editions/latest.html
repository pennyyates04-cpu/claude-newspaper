AI Infrastructure Debt Fears Hit CoreWeave and Oracle as Rate-Hike Odds Climb

On October 14–15, a hotter-than-expected September Producer Price Index report increased bets on a Federal Reserve rate hike, triggering a selloff in heavily indebted AI infrastructure companies. CoreWeave's stock fell to $60.36 on October 15 — its lowest intraday price since May 2025 — after two days of double-digit declines, with yields on some of its bonds briefly touching 15% and credit-default-swap spreads (the market cost of insuring against default) surging past 1,000 basis points, up from roughly 600 the day before. Oracle shares fell alongside it, and Oracle's own five-year credit-default-swap spread widened to a record 288 basis points. This is the clearest sign yet that investors are starting to price in real credit risk around the AI infrastructure boom, not just equity volatility — and Oracle, NetSuite's parent company, is now part of that story even though NetSuite's ERP business has nothing to do with AI data-center debt.

Sales angle
If a prospect mentions hearing "Oracle is in trouble," this is useful context — it's investor anxiety about Oracle's AI cloud infrastructure bets and debt load, not about NetSuite's ERP business or financial stability.
SDR takeaway
A credit-default swap (CDS) is essentially insurance against a company defaulting on its debt; when CDS spreads widen sharply, the market is pricing in a higher chance of default — a leading indicator worth understanding when prospects raise vendor financial health.

Anthropic Launches "Cyber Mission" to Secure Critical Infrastructure With Claude

On October 8, Anthropic introduced the "Cyber Mission," a long-term effort to give defenders tools to secure critical infrastructure and open-source software. Its first piece, the Critical Infrastructure Defense Program, partners Anthropic with 11 companies — including Accenture, CrowdStrike, Deloitte, Palo Alto Networks, PwC, and Rockwell Automation — to give operators of power grids, water systems, and transportation networks access to frontier Claude models, on-site engineers, and Anthropic's threat research. Alongside it, Anthropic introduced a free, opt-in "OSS Scanner" that runs Claude-powered security scans on open-source projects and sends back bug reports — including a proof-of-concept exploit and a suggested fix — without human review, targeting a true-positive rate above 90%. It's one of the clearest signs yet that AI vendors are moving from chatbots into the operational security of physical infrastructure — a trend every enterprise software vendor, NetSuite included, will increasingly be asked about as "AI governance" and "AI security" become standard parts of vendor evaluations.

Sales angle
Cybersecurity and compliance come up constantly with mid-market and enterprise prospects; this is a concrete example to point to when a prospect asks how seriously AI vendors are taking security risk.
SDR takeaway
A "proof of concept" (PoC) in security means a working demonstration that a vulnerability can actually be exploited, not just a theoretical flaw — distinguishing real risk from noise is useful in both security and sales conversations.

HubSpot Cuts Nearly 660 Jobs in AI-Tied Reorganization

On October 6, HubSpot told employees it is cutting nearly 660 jobs — about 7% of its workforce — after its board approved the restructuring on October 1. CEO Yamini Rangan said in an internal memo that the company needs to "fundamentally change the way we are organized to compete and win," reorganizing product teams around customer outcomes instead of individual product "Hubs" and removing layers of management. Rangan said the cuts are not simply a cost-cutting move or driven by AI efficiencies, even though the reorganization is tied to HubSpot's broader AI push. The company expects restructuring charges of $65–75 million, and affected employees will get severance of up to 30 weeks' pay plus extended health benefits. HubSpot is a direct competitor in the small-and-midmarket CRM and marketing space, and a 7% workforce cut alongside an AI-driven reorganization is a sign that well-known SaaS vendors are restructuring around AI faster than their own messaging admits — a pattern worth watching across NetSuite's competitive landscape too.

Sales angle
If a HubSpot (or HubSpot-adjacent) prospect mentions internal reorganization or team changes, this context explains why — and it may be a good moment to ask how stable their current tooling and support feels right now.
SDR takeaway
Companies often frame layoffs as "organizational redesign" rather than "AI replacing jobs" — both can be true at once, and it's worth reading restructuring announcements skeptically rather than taking the stated reason at face value.
Conversation starter

Intuit Pushes "Intuit Intelligence" AI Tools Into Canada, UK, and Australia

In mid-October, Intuit expanded "Intuit Intelligence," the AI tools it first launched for U.S. QuickBooks customers, into Canada, the UK, and Australia, rolling out in beta for QuickBooks Online and QuickBooks Online Advanced. Intuit says the tools go beyond answering questions — they can complete bookkeeping tasks directly inside QuickBooks, such as categorizing transactions, surfacing cash-flow insights, and recommending next steps, while leaving final decisions to the user. Intuit also pointed to its 2026 AI Impact Report, which found trust, not cost, is now the biggest barrier to small businesses adopting AI more widely. QuickBooks is one of NetSuite's most direct competitors at the small-business end of the market, and embedding "do it for you" AI agents into day-to-day bookkeeping — not just chat — is the same agentic-AI pitch every ERP and accounting vendor, NetSuite included, is racing to make credible to finance teams.

Sales angle

Goldman Sachs Beats Q3 Estimates as Equities Trading Revenue Jumps 72%

Goldman Sachs reported Q3 2026 results on October 13: net earnings of about $5.29 billion, up roughly 29% year-over-year, with diluted earnings per share of $17.01 — well above the roughly $13 analysts expected. Net revenue came in around $18.68 billion, up 23%, driven largely by equities trading revenue of $6.44 billion, up 72% from a year earlier on strength in derivatives and financing. Investment banking fees rose about 21% to $3.22 billion, while fixed-income trading revenue was roughly flat. Goldman is one of the first major banks to report each quarter, and a beat this large — powered by trading and dealmaking rather than cost controls — is read as a signal that corporate deal activity and capital markets stayed healthy through the summer, which matters for any prospect in financial services or a capital-markets-adjacent industry.

Sales angle

Hot Producer Prices Push Fed Rate-Hike Odds Higher

The September Producer Price Index, released October 15, came in far hotter than expected: producer prices rose 1.3% month-over-month (versus a 0.6% forecast) and 6.3% year-over-year, while core PPI (which excludes food and energy) rose 0.7% after a 0.2% increase in August. The report pushed market odds of a Federal Reserve rate hike at its October 27–28 meeting to roughly 34%, up from about 29% before the data, and raised the odds of a December hike to around 63%, according to CME's FedWatch tool. The Fed has been raising rates to fight inflation rather than cutting them, the opposite of what most businesses expected a year ago — higher-for-longer rates raise borrowing costs for everything from AI data-center buildouts (see the CoreWeave story above) to ordinary working-capital loans, exactly the kind of macro pressure that shows up in prospects' budget conversations.

Sales angle
A prospect who says their company is being "more careful with cash" right now has real macro backing — rising rate-hike odds are a legitimate reason for tighter budgets, not just corporate caution-speak.
SDR takeaway
PPI measures price changes at the wholesale/producer level (what businesses pay), while CPI measures what consumers pay — PPI often moves first and can be an early signal of where consumer inflation, and Fed policy, is headed.
Conversation starter
"With producer prices running hotter than expected and rate-hike odds climbing again, has that shown up yet in how your company is budgeting for next year?"
