Workday Launches "Autopilot" Agents That Finish Whole Tasks, Not Just Assist

At its Rising 2026 conference in Las Vegas (October 12–15), Workday announced on October 13 a new set of "Autopilot" AI agents built to complete real work across HR, finance, and IT rather than simply assist a human doing it. The first one targets recruiting: it drafts candidate outreach and builds shortlists for a recruiter to approve. It's available to early-adopter customers now, with Workday targeting broader general availability in early 2027. Workday is one of NetSuite's most direct competitors in HR and finance software, and this marks a concrete shift from "AI assistant" positioning toward AI that finishes a task end-to-end — a framing an increasing number of enterprise software vendors are racing to adopt.

Workday Opens Its Platform to Outside AI Agents, Partners With Anthropic

Also on October 13 at Rising, Workday announced its Trusted Agent Platform is now "headless" — letting customers build and run their own AI agents with governed access to Workday's HR and finance data, instead of only using Workday-built agents inside Workday's own interface. Components including Agent-Ready Tools, Workday Data Cloud, a Developer Agent, Custom Agents, and an Agent Harness are now generally available. Workday also announced a partnership with Anthropic, including a plugin that lets Claude work with Workday's new Sana-powered home screen; it's in early access now, with general availability expected later in 2026. Opening HR and finance data to externally built, governed AI agents — rather than keeping agents locked inside one vendor's own interface — is a meaningfully different architecture bet than most ERP/HCM vendors have made publicly, and the Anthropic tie-up signals how enterprise software vendors are choosing AI model partners.

Most Companies Running AI Agents Aren't Measuring Whether They Work, Liferay Survey Finds

A Liferay-commissioned survey of 500 US professionals involved in AI decisions or day-to-day AI use, fielded via Pollfish and released August 12, 2026, found that 54% of companies are running AI agents in production or actively piloting them. But only 25% consistently measure AI's impact against defined KPIs, and just 24% have a company-wide AI usage policy. Technology companies reported the highest adoption at 72%, followed by healthcare (66%) and transportation/logistics (64%); the most common use cases were data analysis and reporting (35%) and customer support (32%). It's a concrete data point on a gap showing up across most 2026 AI-adoption research: companies are deploying AI agents faster than they're building the governance and measurement practices to know whether those agents are actually working.

Sales angle
If a prospect's team is excited about AI agents but can't point to how they're measuring results, that's a legitimate, non-pushy opening to talk about how a system like NetSuite ties AI features to measurable financial and operational outcomes rather than agent activity for its own sake.
SDR takeaway
"54% adoption" headlines are common in 2026 AI surveys, but the more useful number underneath is usually the measurement gap — a reminder to ask prospects how they'd know if an AI tool were actually helping, not just whether they're using one.
Conversation starter
