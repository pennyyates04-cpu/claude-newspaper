Gartner: Worldwide IT Spending to Grow 15% in 2027, Topping $7.35 Trillion for the First Time

What happened: Gartner forecast on October 21 that worldwide IT spending will reach about $7.35 trillion in 2027, up 15% from 2026 — the first time the total crosses $7 trillion. Data center systems is the fastest-growing category, projected to grow roughly 55.5% to about $1.37 trillion, driven by AI infrastructure buildouts. IaaS is projected to grow about 25.7%, software about 14.6%, devices about 8.1%, IT services about 4.9%, and communications services about 2.9%. Gartner says hardware markets face cost pressure from memory-related price inflation even as AI drives most of the growth. Why it matters: Software spending growing nearly 15% — one of the fastest categories outside the AI data-center buildout itself — is a direct tailwind for enterprise software budgets heading into next year.

Sales angle
Finance and IT leaders are actively building 2027 budgets right now, and Gartner's numbers say AI investment is the main thing reshaping those budgets — a timely, data-backed reason to ask about a prospect's own planning.
SDR takeaway
"Net-new spend" in forecasts like this one refers to the dollar increase over the prior year, not total spend — useful when a prospect or report cites a growth figure rather than an absolute number.

Workday and Anthropic Partner to Wire Claude's Reasoning Into HR and Finance Workflows

What happened: At Workday Rising on October 13, Workday and Anthropic announced a partnership connecting Claude to Sana, Workday's AI agent and orchestration layer, and to Workday's underlying HR and finance data. The two systems pass information and tasks through the Model Context Protocol (MCP), an open standard, so employees can start work in either Claude or Sana and keep the same context. Workday says its existing permissions, approval requirements, and audit controls continue to apply to any request made through Claude. "Sana in Claude" is live for early-access customers now, with general availability expected in late 2026. Why it matters: It's a concrete example of a major enterprise software vendor embedding a frontier AI model's reasoning directly into day-to-day business workflows while keeping the underlying data and governance inside its own system — a pattern NetSuite and other ERP/HCM vendors will likely need to match.

Sales angle
A prospect currently on Workday may soon start hearing about "Claude in Workday" or Sana from their account team — understanding what it actually does (and doesn't) helps position NetSuite's own AI roadmap credibly in that conversation.
SDR takeaway
The Model Context Protocol (MCP) is an open standard that lets an AI assistant like Claude plug into a business application's data and tools without a custom integration for each connection — it's becoming the common "wiring" enterprise AI agents use to reach business systems.
Conversation starter
"Workday just connected Claude's reasoning directly into their HR and finance workflows through an open standard called MCP — has your team looked at how AI agents are being wired into the systems you already use, versus a separate standalone chatbot?"

NetSuite's Own Conference Opens This Week as the "NetSuite Next" Rollout Continues

What happened: SuiteWorld 2026, Oracle NetSuite's annual customer and partner conference, runs October 25–28 in Las Vegas. It arrives as Oracle continues a phased rollout of "NetSuite Next," an AI-centered redesign of the ERP built around "Ask Oracle" — a conversational assistant that can analyze business data, answer questions in plain language, surface insights, and recommend actions from inside the existing NetSuite interface. Oracle announced the rollout on July 15 with its 2026.2 release, starting with US and Canada accounts; the feature isn't switched on automatically, and customers wait for an Oracle rollout notice before an administrator can request access. Additional countries are expected to follow. Why it matters: It's the clearest signal yet of where NetSuite is taking its core product — AI assistance built into the ERP itself rather than a bolt-on tool — and SuiteWorld is where Oracle NetSuite leadership frames that roadmap to its full customer and partner base this week.

Sales angle
Being able to speak accurately about NetSuite Next and Ask Oracle — what's live today for US/Canada customers versus still rolling out elsewhere — is directly useful when a prospect asks how NetSuite's AI compares to moves like SAP's Joule Work or Workday's new Claude integration.
SDR takeaway

Corporate-Spend Fintech Ramp Raises $1.85 Billion at a $60 Billion Valuation

What happened: Ramp, a corporate card and spend-management fintech, announced a $1.85 billion primary financing round led by Dragoneer Investment Group, with Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Sands Capital, and Avenir Growth also participating. The round values Ramp at $60 billion, up sharply from the $44 billion valuation it carried just four months earlier, when it raised $750 million in June 2026. Law firm Latham & Watkins, which advised Ramp on the deal, published the announcement in October. Why it matters: It's one of the largest fintech or SaaS raises of the year, a sign investors are still committing large sums to vertical, AI-enabled finance software even as broader tech funding grows more selective — and it sits squarely in NetSuite's competitive orbit, since corporate card and spend management increasingly overlaps with ERP.

Sales angle

NRF Forecasts Record $1.013 Trillion Holiday Season, Up 4.2% From Last Year

What happened: The National Retail Federation forecast on October 15 that US holiday retail sales in November and December will rise 4.2% over 2025, to $1.013 trillion — the first time the forecast has crossed $1 trillion. That compares with 3.1% growth to $972 billion last year. NRF Chief Economist Mark Mathews said roughly half of the projected growth should come from real increases in spending rather than higher prices, with higher-income shoppers expected to drive much of the season. Retailers are expected to hire between 325,000 and 375,000 seasonal workers, roughly in line with last year's estimated 356,000. NRF's figure excludes automobile dealers, gas stations, and restaurants. Why it matters: It's the clearest read yet on how retailers — and the wholesale and distribution businesses that supply them — are sizing up the most important quarter of their year, which shapes how much they can spend on systems like point-of-sale, inventory, and ERP once the season ends.

Sales angle
Retail and e-commerce prospects are heads-down on execution right now, not evaluating new systems — this is a moment to understand their Q4 pressures and build the relationship ahead of a Q1 budget conversation, not to push for a decision.
SDR takeaway
NRF's holiday figure is a "core retail" number — it leaves out auto dealers, gas stations, and restaurants — so it doesn't capture a prospect's full revenue if they sell in one of those categories.
Conversation starter
"NRF is projecting a record holiday season this year, but retailers are still being cautious on seasonal hiring — how is your team balancing growth expectations with cost control heading into Q4?"
