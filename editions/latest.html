SAP Tops €10 Billion in Quarterly Revenue for the First Time, With Cloud Growth Accelerating to 25%

SAP reported Q3 2026 results on October 21: revenue rose about 12% to €10.3 billion (roughly $11.6 billion), its first quarter ever above €10 billion, with cloud revenue up 25% to €6.7 billion. Non-IFRS EPS came in at €1.89, beating estimates of roughly €1.83. SAP reaffirmed full-year guidance, with cloud revenue trending toward the top of its €25.8–26.2 billion range, while flagging a tougher year-ago comparison as a risk to its 2027 growth target. Shares rose after the report. SAP is NetSuite's largest direct ERP competitor in the enterprise and upper-mid-market, and a record revenue quarter signals large companies are still funding cloud ERP migrations despite broader macro caution elsewhere in tech.

Netflix Posts Record 34.6% Operating Margin in Q3, Beats Its Own Forecast

Netflix reported Q3 2026 results on October 20: revenue grew 12% year-over-year, in line with its own forecast, while operating income reached $4.5 billion and operating margin hit 34.6%, up sharply from 28.2% a year earlier. Diluted EPS was $0.87, versus $0.59 in Q3 2025. For Q4, Netflix guided to $13.4 billion in revenue and a 26.0% operating margin — including a roughly 120-basis-point hit from recent headcount reductions — and projected full-year revenue of $51.1 billion, up 13%. Netflix is often read as a bellwether for discretionary consumer spending, and a record-margin quarter suggests revenue is still growing even as the company cuts costs elsewhere — a reminder that layoffs and growth are increasingly happening at the same companies at once.

Sales angle

SDR takeaway

New G2 Research: B2B Buyers Increasingly Start With AI Chatbots, But Few Trust AI to Close the Deal

G2's 2026 "Evaluation Maze" buyer behavior report, published July 22 and based on a survey of more than 1,000 B2B software buyers, found that 82% sourced software recommendations from an AI chatbot in the past two years. Yet only 9% said they're comfortable letting AI agents execute a purchase within approved guardrails, and just 2% would let AI buy without pre-approval. The report also found the evaluation stage of the buying journey has gotten longer, as internal resistance, finance oversight, and low trust in autonomous AI purchasing stretch out decisions. This is a direct, data-backed look at the gap between "AI is reshaping how buyers research" and "AI isn't replacing human judgment on actual purchase decisions" — worth knowing given AI sales hype in both directions.

Sales angle
If a prospect is deep in AI-assisted research on their own (comparing NetSuite to competitors via chatbot tools), that's normal and expected — but the data says the actual decision, especially anything involving spend approval, still runs through humans, so the SDR's job of building trust with a real decision-maker hasn't gone away.
SDR takeaway
"Evaluation stage" is the part of a buying process where a prospect compares vendors in depth (demos, trials, references) before deciding — G2's data says this stage is getting longer, not shorter, despite more AI-assisted research upfront.
