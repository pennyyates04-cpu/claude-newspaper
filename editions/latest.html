Gartner Raises 2026 Global AI Spending Forecast to $2.7 Trillion, Up 49.5%

On September 16, Gartner raised its forecast for worldwide AI spending to about $2.7 trillion in 2026 (up 49.5% from 2025) and roughly $3.6 trillion in 2027. It also raised its 2026 growth outlook for AI application-development platforms from 28% to 39%, citing AI infrastructure demand that's stayed strong despite rising memory-chip prices. Gartner's spending forecasts are one of the most widely cited independent benchmarks of enterprise AI investment, and an upward revision this large signals that AI buying is still accelerating rather than leveling off — which shapes how every enterprise software vendor, NetSuite's competitors included, pitches "AI" as a budget line item to CFOs and CIOs.

Sales angle
When a prospect says their company is "being cautious on AI spend," this forecast is useful context that the broader market is moving the other way — a good opening to ask what's actually driving their caution rather than assuming it reflects the industry.
SDR takeaway
Gartner splits AI spending into infrastructure (chips, servers, data centers), software, and services; infrastructure is still the largest category, but software and services are growing fastest — worth knowing which bucket a prospect's "AI budget" actually falls into.

Morgan Stanley Beats Q3 2026 Estimates on Strong Trading and Wealth Management

That topped the $2.93-per-share consensus analysts had expected. Morgan Stanley is among the first major banks to report each quarter, and a beat driven by trading and wealth management — not cost-cutting — is read as a sign that corporate deal activity and capital markets stayed healthy through the summer, relevant context for any prospect whose business touches financing, M&A, or wealth clients.

Cold Calls Still Beat AI-Written Emails 6-to-1, New ValueSelling Study Finds

A September 2026 survey from ValueSelling Group found that among B2B sales leaders, referrals were rated the most effective prospecting method, with cold calls second at 47% — compared with just 8% for AI-written personalized emails. The same survey found 46% of sales leaders say reps give up on prospects too quickly, 40% cite difficulty handling objections, and 39% say reps are simply afraid to pick up the phone. It's a direct, current data point against the assumption that AI tools have replaced old-school prospecting — human-led outreach, especially the phone call, still outperforms AI-generated alternatives in sales leaders' own rankings, even as AI tool adoption keeps climbing elsewhere in the sales stack.

Sales angle
This is a useful reminder for your own prospecting mix: AI tools are good for research, list-building, and drafting, but the data says they're a poor substitute for a referral or a real phone conversation when it comes to actually landing a response.
SDR takeaway
"Objection handling" is responding to a prospect's stated reason for not moving forward (price, timing, no perceived need) in a way that keeps the conversation open — the survey's finding that 40% of leaders see this as a weak spot is worth remembering as a coaching area.
Conversation starter

AI Still Soaking Up Venture Capital Even as Overall Deal Value Drops, PitchBook Finds

The PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor for Q3 2026, released October 8, found U.S. venture deal value hit a record $515.8 billion for the first nine months of 2026 — about 44% above the previous full-year record — but the total is skewed by a handful of giant AI rounds: OpenAI and Anthropic alone raised more than $200 billion combined in the first half. Q3 alone saw deal value fall roughly 40% from Q2, to $98.4 billion, as megadeal activity cooled and the exits (IPOs, acquisitions) needed to return money to investors kept lagging. It's a sign the venture market is increasingly bifurcated — a small number of frontier AI labs are absorbing most of the capital, while everyone else, including many SaaS and mid-market startups that could become NetSuite prospects, is fundraising in a much tighter market than the eye-popping headline totals suggest.

Sales angle
A startup prospect who isn't a frontier AI lab may be facing a tougher fundraising environment than "record VC funding" headlines suggest — worth asking directly about their runway and growth plans rather than assuming they have easy access to capital.
SDR takeaway
"Deal value" counts the dollars invested, not the number of deals — a market can set a dollar record while most companies still struggle to raise, because a handful of giant rounds skew the total.
