## Oracle Sends "Force Majeure" Notice on Its New Mexico AI Data Center

**What happened:** On September 24, 2026, Oracle sent a force majeure notice to Blue Owl Capital, developer of "Project Jupiter," a 2.45-gigawatt AI data center campus under construction in New Mexico as part of the Stargate initiative. The notice would let Oracle delay payments if the facility misses its 2028 target, and follows energy-supply setbacks: a gas pipeline meant to fuel the site's Bloom Energy fuel cells has slipped nearly six months, to February 1, 2027, after regulators repeatedly denied permits, and a separate air-quality permit is still pending. Oracle says the project "remains on our planned schedule" and it isn't trying to exit as the site's main tenant; Blue Owl says the notice "does not change the financial commitments to this multi-year project." Oracle shares fell roughly 3% on the news.
**Why it matters:** It's a concrete sign that the buildout behind Oracle's AI cloud promises — the same roughly $250 billion in committed AI-datacenter spending that got its credit rating cut this summer — is hitting real-world bottlenecks like permitting and power delivery, not just financing.

**Sales angle:** Useful context if a prospect raises Oracle's AI infrastructure ambitions — a reminder that the "AI supercycle" still depends on unglamorous things like gas pipelines and permits, and delays there can ripple into cloud capacity and pricing.
**SDR takeaway:** A "force majeure" clause lets a party pause or excuse contractual obligations (like payments) due to events outside its control; invoking it doesn't necessarily mean a project is failing, but it is a formal signal that a company is protecting itself against delay risk.
**Conversation starter:** "I saw Oracle had to formally flag delays at one of its big AI data center builds over power and permitting — has your team run into any infrastructure or capacity bottlenecks as you've scaled up AI usage?"

## Dataiku Launches a Product Just to Track Everyone Else's AI Agents

**What happened:** On September 24, 2026, Dataiku launched Agent Management, a standalone product that scans an organization's AI agents across platforms — including AWS Bedrock, Databricks, Google Vertex, Microsoft Copilot Studio, Salesforce Agentforce, and Snowflake Cortex — to build a single inventory, measure business and technical performance, and flag which agents pose the greatest risk. Agents that touch customers, sensitive data, or live transactions get a standing certification record, named risks, and scheduled retests. The launch cites IBM research finding fewer than one in five organizations keep a complete, current inventory of their AI systems. It becomes generally available in October, priced per instance annually with monitoring metered per agent.
**Why it matters:** As companies adopt AI agents from multiple vendors at once, a "who's-in-charge-of-this-agent" gap is emerging — one of the first dedicated products to treat agent sprawl as its own governance problem, cutting across every individual ERP or CRM vendor's own agent platform.

**Sales angle:** A good discovery question for any prospect already running Agentforce, Copilot, or similar tools: fragmented, ungoverned AI agents across departments is a real pain point that a more unified platform can reduce, simply by not adding another disconnected silo.
**SDR takeaway:** "Agent sprawl" describes what happens when different teams adopt AI agents from different vendors without central oversight — similar to the "shadow IT" problem from the SaaS era, but for autonomous AI.
**Conversation starter:** "I saw a company just launched a product whose whole job is inventorying and risk-scoring an organization's AI agents, because most companies don't actually know what agents they're running — is that visibility something your team has today?"

## Salesforce and Siemens Plug Agentforce Directly Into Engineering Data

**What happened:** On September 15, 2026 at Dreamforce, Salesforce and Siemens announced an expanded partnership integrating Salesforce's Agentforce AI agents with Siemens' Teamcenter Service Lifecycle Management software, giving sales and service AI agents direct access to Siemens' engineering "digital twin" data. In practice, a technician's AI agent can identify the correct spare part for a specific machine's serial number before a site visit, and a rep's AI agent can verify whether a proposed upgrade is technically viable and manufacturable, without waiting on a manual reply from engineering. Siemens says it already uses Agentforce to process more than 2,500 unqualified leads a month across 132 countries for its 18,000 sellers.
**Why it matters:** It's a concrete example of a CRM vendor pushing AI agents deeper into industrial and manufacturing operations — a vertical where engineering and inventory data usually live in the ERP system, not the CRM — showing how the ERP/CRM boundary keeps blurring as agentic AI spreads.

**Sales angle:** Manufacturing prospects evaluating Salesforce are increasingly being sold on agents that reach into engineering and product data — relevant when discussing whether a prospect's ERP (inventory, bill of materials, service parts data) is structured well enough to support that kind of integration, regardless of vendor.
**SDR takeaway:** A "digital twin" is a live virtual model of a physical product or machine, used to track its real specifications and history — connecting AI agents to that data is what lets them give accurate, machine-specific answers instead of generic ones.
**Conversation starter:** "I saw Salesforce and Siemens are connecting AI sales agents directly to engineering data so reps get instant, machine-specific answers — is your team's product or inventory data structured well enough today to support something like that?"
