Azure Jumps 48%, AWS Hits 39% Growth as Cloud Giants Show No Sign of Slowing AI Spending

Microsoft's fiscal Q1 2027 results, reported October 28, showed revenue of $92.3 billion, up 19% year over year and above the $90.75 billion analysts expected. For the first time, Microsoft broke out Azure's dollar revenue rather than just its growth rate: $33.2 billion, up 48%. The next day, Amazon reported Q3 2026 revenue of $201.9 billion — slightly below the $202.3 billion Wall Street expected, with EPS of $1.89 missing the roughly $1.96–$1.97 consensus — but AWS revenue grew 39.3%, its fastest pace in nearly five years, and operating income of $28.1 billion beat the high end of Amazon's own guidance. Both cloud divisions are now growing faster than they have in years — evidence that enterprise AI workloads are converting into real, booked infrastructure revenue rather than just capex promises, even as investors start asking how long that spending can continue.

Alphabet Shares Sink About 11% Despite 25% Revenue Growth, as AI Spending Pushes Free Cash Flow Negative Again

Alphabet's Q3 2026 results, reported October 28, showed revenue of about $127.6 billion, up roughly 25% year over year, with Google Cloud revenue surging to around $30.7 billion — more than double a year earlier. But diluted EPS of $2.12 missed expectations, partly due to an $8.7 billion loss on equity investments, and capex of about $51.4 billion drove a second straight quarter of negative free cash flow. Shares fell roughly 11% the next session. It's a sign investors are growing more anxious about how long Big Tech can keep increasing AI infrastructure spending without the cash generation to match it — strong growth alone isn't reassuring the market if the spending-to-cash-flow math looks unsustainable.

NetSuite 2026.2 Brings Ask Oracle's Conversational AI Assistant Into the Core NetSuite Experience

Oracle's NetSuite 2026.2 release, rolling out to accounts between August and October 2026, extends "Ask Oracle" — the conversational AI assistant originally built for NetSuite Next — into the standard NetSuite experience, with access controlled by user role. Oracle is launching it first for U.S. and Canada accounts, with other countries planned later. The release also adds passkey sign-in and bank reconciliation and reporting improvements. Oracle is folding its newer NetSuite Next AI capabilities back into the product nearly a million businesses already run, rather than treating AI as a separate, premium-only track — lowering the bar for existing customers to actually start using an AI assistant day to day.

SAP to Acquire Belgian AI Startup TechWolf to Feed Real-Time Skills Data Into Joule and SuccessFactors

SAP announced on October 6 a definitive agreement to acquire TechWolf, a Ghent, Belgium-based "work intelligence" startup whose software maps the tasks, skills, and labor-market signals behind every job. Terms weren't disclosed. SAP plans to fold TechWolf's technology into SuccessFactors and its Joule AI agents, saying the data will make AI responses more accurate and cheaper to run. The deal is expected to close in Q4 2026 pending regulatory approval; TechWolf will keep its own brand, with its co-founder staying on as CEO. SAP is betting that generic AI agents need real, current data about what employees actually do to be useful for HR and workforce decisions — another case of an enterprise software vendor buying specialized data and context rather than building it from scratch.

Sales angle
A prospect using SAP SuccessFactors may soon see more precise AI-driven skills and workforce insights baked into their HR system — a useful comparison point when discussing how deeply any vendor's AI features are informed by a customer's actual operational data.
SDR takeaway
"Work intelligence" or "skills graph" software maps what people actually do in their jobs, not just their job titles — increasingly treated as foundational data for making workforce-focused AI agents more accurate.

ServiceNow Beats Q3 Estimates Again, Launches "AI Passport" to Govern What Its AI Agents Can Access

ServiceNow's Q3 2026 results, reported October 28, showed subscription revenue of $3.99 billion, up 21% year over year, beating the high end of its own guidance and prompting another raise to its full-year outlook. Alongside the results, ServiceNow highlighted "AI Passport," a tool for checking and controlling what its AI agents can access or execute, plus a no-code environment ("ToolSmith") for employees to build their own agents. "AI agent governance" — controlling what autonomous software is allowed to touch — has become as central to enterprise software vendors' pitches as the agents' raw capabilities, following similar moves from Workday and others this year.

Forrester: Procurement Now Drives More Than Half of B2B Buying Decisions, and Still Doesn't Fully Trust AI Research

Forrester's 2026 State of Business Buying research finds procurement professionals are decision-makers in 53% of B2B buying cycles and typically engage from the start of the process, not just at contract-signing. The same research found buyers use generative AI tools to research vendors but don't fully trust the results: 28% of procurement respondents said they felt less confident in a decision because of inaccurate AI-generated output, compared with 19% of buyers overall. Even as AI chatbots become a common first stop in vendor research, the people who actually sign off on enterprise deals are more numerous, more involved earlier, and more skeptical of AI-generated shortcuts than sales teams might assume.
