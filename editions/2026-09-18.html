Amazon Locks In $8 Billion Generator Deal With Generac to Power AI Data Centers

On September 16, 2026, Generac Holdings disclosed a long-term supply agreement with Amazon worth up to $8 billion to provide backup power generators for Amazon's data centers, per an SEC filing. Generac expects to deliver about $2.4 billion of generators in 2027-2028. Amazon also received warrants for up to 1,693,745 Generac shares at $200.93 each, vesting as Generac delivers on the contract. Generac shares jumped sharply on the news. It's the latest example of a hyperscaler locking in a hardware supplier with an equity-linked, multi-year contract to secure backup power for AI data centers — a sign of how much physical infrastructure spending is now tied to AI buildouts, not just chips and cloud capacity.

SDR takeaway

Cisco Rushes Emergency Patch for Maximum-Severity, Already-Exploited ISE Flaw

On September 16, 2026, Cisco issued an emergency advisory for CVE-2026-76460, a maximum-severity (CVSS 10.0) authentication-bypass flaw in Cisco Identity Services Engine (ISE) and ISE Passive Identity Connector, versions 3.0-3.5. An unauthenticated attacker can send a crafted request to bypass the web-based management interface and gain unauthorized access; Cisco said the flaw was already being exploited before the patch shipped. CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day, giving federal agencies until September 19 to patch. Fixed releases are 3.1 Patch 12, 3.2 Patch 11, 3.3 Patch 12, 3.4 Patch 7, and 3.5 Patch 4; there is no workaround beyond restricting network access. ISE is widely used to control network access at mid-market and enterprise companies, so a maximum-severity, actively exploited bug in it is a live risk for many NetSuite prospects' IT teams this week, not just background patch news.

Sales angle
For a prospect using Cisco networking gear, this is a timely, genuine reason to ask how they track and patch critical vendor vulnerabilities, especially ones already under active attack.
SDR takeaway
CVSS is the 0–10 industry severity scale for vulnerabilities; 10.0 is the maximum, reserved for flaws that are both trivial to exploit and hand an attacker full control — worth recognizing as a red flag, not routine patch news.

Microsoft Copilot Adds "Bring Your Own Knowledge" Connectors for SAP and Salesforce Data

Microsoft 365 Copilot has begun rolling out "bring your own knowledge" connectors in targeted preview, letting enterprises surface data from non-Microsoft systems — including SAP (ECC, S/4HANA, SuccessFactors), Salesforce, ServiceNow, and custom REST APIs — directly inside Copilot's grounding context. The connectors use the same schema as Microsoft Graph connectors plus a new streaming-ingestion API for near-real-time updates; Salesforce data can surface accounts, opportunities, and cases, while SAP data can surface orders, materials, and employee records. Access is currently limited to eligible tenants enrolled through a Microsoft account team. Microsoft is positioning Copilot as a neutral AI layer that sits on top of whatever ERP or CRM a company already runs, rather than pushing customers toward a single Microsoft system — a meaningfully different approach from vendors, including Oracle, that want their AI features to live inside their own product.

Sales angle
If a prospect uses Copilot heavily, they may expect any new software — NetSuite included — to plug into it the same way SAP and Salesforce data now can, which is worth knowing before a demo.
SDR takeaway
"Grounding" is the AI term for connecting a model to real, current company data so its answers reflect actual records instead of generic training knowledge — the core idea behind most enterprise AI connector features.
