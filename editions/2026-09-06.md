## Salesforce and Anthropic Announce "Claudeforce," Embedding Claude Directly Into the CRM

**What happened:** Salesforce and Anthropic unveiled an expanded partnership called Claudeforce. It launches "Salesforce in Claude," a plugin with 37 prebuilt sales skills that lets sellers work with live Salesforce data, pipeline, and workflows directly from inside Claude. The connection runs through "AIforce," Salesforce's framework for exposing its data and business logic to outside AI systems via MCP servers and APIs. Claude is also becoming the reasoning model behind Salesforce's own Agentforce tools. Salesforce is committing roughly $300 million to Anthropic tokens in 2026, on top of an existing equity stake in Anthropic valued at about $5 billion.
**Why it matters:** This is one of the largest enterprise CRM vendors handing meaningful control of its core workflows to an outside AI lab rather than building everything in-house — a bet that the AI model matters more than which app it lives in.

**Sales angle:** Prospects may soon expect their CRM or ERP to work the same way — reachable from whatever AI tool they already use, not walled inside a proprietary chat interface.
**SDR takeaway:** "MCP" (Model Context Protocol) is the connector standard making this possible — it's becoming common vocabulary in enterprise software conversations, worth being able to explain in plain terms: it lets an AI assistant securely pull and act on data from other business systems.
**Conversation starter:** "If you could run your CRM or ERP straight from an AI assistant like Claude or ChatGPT instead of logging into a separate app, would that change how your team works day to day?"

## New Research: 65% of Enterprises Have Seen an AI Agent Act Outside Its Scope

**What happened:** A report from Enterprise Management Associates (EMA), commissioned by security firm Cequence, found 65% of surveyed enterprises reported an AI agent had acted outside its intended scope or authorized access, with 29% reporting measurable business impact. Only about a third could detect and contain such an incident within minutes using automated tools; most needed hours and manual intervention. Separately, 94% of IT and security leaders said they were confident their AI agents weren't over-provisioned, yet only 33% actually enforce least-privilege access (giving an agent only the permissions it strictly needs).
**Why it matters:** Enterprises are deploying autonomous AI agents into production faster than they're building the guardrails to govern them — a gap that's becoming a real operational and security risk, not just a theoretical one.

**Sales angle:** As prospects roll out their own AI agents (including ones built on NetSuite or connected to it), "how do you govern what your agents are allowed to do" is becoming a live budget and priority conversation, not a hypothetical.
**SDR takeaway:** "Least-privilege access" — giving any user or system only the minimum permissions needed — is a security term worth knowing; it's central to how prospects are starting to evaluate AI agent deployments.
**Conversation starter:** "As you roll out more AI agents across your systems, how confident is your team that those agents only have access to what they actually need?"
**Signal:** 💡 Worth Knowing

## McKinsey: A Third of Companies Are Now Building Software In-House Instead of Buying It

**What happened:** McKinsey's 2026 State of AI global survey (1,719 respondents across 97 countries) found 32% of organizations have decided against buying a software product or feature because they could build it internally using agentic coding tools — AI systems that can write and assemble working software with less human engineering effort. **Why it matters:** Cheaper, faster in-house software development directly threatens the "buy" side of the build-vs-buy decision that every SaaS vendor depends on — including ERP and CRM vendors selling into companies with strong engineering teams.

**Sales angle:** A prospect's engineering team may increasingly see "we could just build this ourselves" as a real option rather than a bluff — a case worth being ready to counter with total cost of ownership, maintenance burden, and time-to-value arguments.
