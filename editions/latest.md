## Mistral AI Raises €3 Billion ($3.5B) Series D Led by Samsung, Europe's Largest-Ever Tech Funding Round

**What happened:** French AI lab Mistral AI announced on September 8 a €3 billion ($3.5 billion) Series D round at a post-money valuation above €21 billion. Samsung Electronics led the round, with the EQT-managed Scaleup Europe Fund and existing investor PSG Equity co-leading. New backers include Advent, BlackRock-managed funds, and the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg; existing investors a16z, ASML, and NVIDIA also participated. Mistral calls it the largest equity round ever completed by a European tech company, and says the money will fund compute, research, and international expansion. The company counts more than 125 enterprise customers, including Airbus and HSBC.
**Why it matters:** It's a concrete signal that non-U.S. "sovereign AI" investment is accelerating fast, with a major hardware player betting directly on a foundation-model company rather than just chips.

## Oracle Invokes Force Majeure on $18 Billion "Project Jupiter" Data Center Tied to Its OpenAI Deal

**What happened:** Oracle sent a force majeure notice to Stack Infrastructure, a Blue Owl Capital unit developing its 1,400-acre "Project Jupiter" data center campus in New Mexico — first reported September 24 and since confirmed across multiple outlets. The notice cites delays securing power, a responsibility that falls to Oracle under the lease, after state officials rejected a natural-gas pipeline extension to the site in July. Oracle isn't exiting the project; invoking force majeure lets it keep paying lower "development-stage" rent for longer if the 2028 completion target is missed. Oracle says the project "remains on our planned schedule"; Blue Owl says its own financial commitments are unchanged. The $18 billion campus is part of Oracle's broader infrastructure buildout tied to its deal with OpenAI.
**Why it matters:** It's a concrete example of how hard — and expensive — it's becoming to secure power for AI data centers, even for a company as large as Oracle, and a reminder that Oracle's AI-cloud growth story carries real execution risk.

**Sales angle:** If a prospect is evaluating Oracle Cloud Infrastructure or watching Oracle's broader AI ambitions, this is a useful, concrete data point that infrastructure delivery timelines in AI aren't guaranteed — worth probing how any vendor's AI roadmap commitments are backed operationally.
**SDR takeaway:** "Force majeure" is a contract clause letting a party delay obligations due to events outside its control; here Oracle is using it defensively to buy time on lease payments, not to exit the deal.
**Conversation starter:** "Oracle's run into real power-delivery delays on one of its big AI data center projects — has power or infrastructure availability come up as a constraint in any of your own company's AI plans?"

## Gartner: 69% of B2B Buyers Still Turn to Sales Reps to Validate AI-Generated Research

**What happened:** A Gartner survey released May 20 found that 69% of B2B buyers who use AI during a purchase process turn to a human sales rep to validate the AI-generated insights before deciding, and that buyers now use an average of seven information sources during a typical purchase. The finding follows an earlier Gartner survey in March that found 67% of B2B buyers say they prefer a "rep-free" buying experience in principle, even though 45% reported using AI tools somewhere in a recent purchase.
**Why it matters:** It resolves an apparent contradiction in B2B buying behavior — buyers say they want to self-serve and avoid reps, but the data shows they still need and use reps as a trust-check on AI output, especially before large purchases.

**Sales angle:** A prospect who's already done extensive AI-assisted research before the first call isn't a bad lead — Gartner's data says that's now the norm, and the SDR's real job has shifted toward confirming and correcting what the AI already told them.
**SDR takeaway:** Don't assume a well-informed prospect doesn't need you; "validation" — catching gaps, context, or mistakes in AI-generated vendor comparisons — is becoming a core part of what a sales rep provides.

## Liferay Study: 54% of Companies Are Running AI Agents, But Only 25% Measure Their Impact

**What happened:** A Liferay-commissioned survey of 500 U.S. professionals involved in AI decision-making, published August 12, found that 54% of companies are already running AI agents in production or piloting them — but only 25% measure that impact with clear KPIs, and only 24% have a company-wide AI usage policy. Adoption varies sharply by industry, from 72% in technology down to 24% in education.
**Why it matters:** It's a current data point behind a pattern many prospects are quietly living through — rapid AI agent rollout outpacing the governance and measurement needed to prove it's actually working.

**Sales angle:** A prospect that's deployed AI agents but can't show ROI from them is a real opportunity — frame NetSuite's AI capabilities around measurable financial and operational outcomes, not just "we have agents too."
**SDR takeaway:** "KPIs" (key performance indicators) are the specific, agreed-upon metrics used to judge whether a project succeeded — without them, a company can't actually prove an AI investment paid off, however it feels.
**Conversation starter:** "A recent study found most companies running AI agents aren't actually measuring their impact yet — is that something your team has a clear way to track, or is it still early days?"
