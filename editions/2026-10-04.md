## Micron's AI-driven memory boom keeps beating estimates

**What happened:** Micron reported fiscal Q4 2026 revenue of $54.23 billion, above the $51.07 billion analysts expected, with adjusted earnings of $33.42 per share versus a $31.61 estimate. Data center revenue roughly quadrupled year-over-year, DRAM revenue topped $100 billion for the fiscal year, and Micron guided next quarter to about $61.5 billion in revenue and $38.15 in adjusted EPS — both above Street expectations. **Why it matters:** Micron makes the memory chips that feed AI data centers, so its results are a real-time read on how much the industry is actually spending to build out AI infrastructure — and demand is still outrunning supply.

**SDR takeaway:** "DRAM" (the memory chips used for short-term data access) pricing is currently spiking due to AI demand — a useful data point if a prospect mentions rising cloud or hardware costs.

## Salesforce debuts "Koa," its first CRM reasoning model, built with NVIDIA

**What happened:** At Dreamforce 2026, Salesforce introduced Koa, described as its first CRM reasoning model for Agentforce, built on NVIDIA's Nemotron technology for agent reasoning, tool use, and multi-step decision-making. Salesforce says Koa trains entirely on synthetic data rather than real customer data, and the company owns and runs the model's weights on its own infrastructure. **SDR takeaway:** A "reasoning model" is built to plan and execute multi-step tasks (not just generate text) — that distinction is becoming the main way vendors differentiate their AI agent offerings.
