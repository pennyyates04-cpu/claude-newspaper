Oracle Proposes New Power Deal and Customer Bill Credits for Its New Mexico AI Mega-Campus

On October 13, Oracle proposed an energy service agreement with utility El Paso Electric to support the next phase of its Project Jupiter AI data-center campus in Doña Ana County, New Mexico — covering late-stage construction, startup power, and backup/life-safety needs, with Oracle saying it could spend at least $100 million on power there over 10 years. Oracle also proposed up to $55 million a year, for up to three years, in electricity bill credits for the utility's New Mexico residential and small-business customers, and reaffirmed that an on-site Bloom Energy fuel-cell microgrid (up to 2.45 gigawatts) — not new gas turbines — remains the campus's primary power source. Separately, Oracle is seeking up to 2 gigawatts of new solar, wind, and geothermal power for 2027–2031 delivery. Oracle keeps revising the design and financing of its roughly $165 billion, 1,400-acre Project Jupiter campus in response to local pushback over water and emissions, and this is the clearest sign yet that Oracle — NetSuite's parent company — will pay nearby communities directly to keep the project moving, a preview of how AI infrastructure buildouts are reshaping Oracle's capital spending ahead of SuiteWorld 2026 later this month.

Sales angle
Oracle's AI-driven capex and public infrastructure fights are a live example of what "AI infrastructure investment" costs in practice — useful context if a prospect asks how committed Oracle is to its AI roadmap, or brings up Oracle's debt and credit-rating headlines.
SDR takeaway
A "fuel-cell microgrid" generates power on-site through an electrochemical reaction rather than combustion, producing fewer emissions than a traditional gas turbine — a term likely to resurface as more enterprise tech companies site AI data centers near power-constrained grids.

TSMC Posts Record Quarter as AI Chip Demand Keeps Outrunning Supply

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. reported third-quarter 2026 results on October 15: consolidated revenue of NT$1.49 trillion (about $46.7 billion), up 50.9% in local currency from a year earlier, with net income up 68.7% and a 66.4% gross margin. Advanced technologies (7-nanometer and smaller) made up 79% of wafer revenue, including a fast-ramping 2-nanometer node. TSMC guided fourth-quarter revenue to $52.3–$54.3 billion, above what analysts had expected. TSMC manufactures the advanced chips behind most of the generative-AI boom — Nvidia, AMD, Apple, and others all depend on it — so a result this far above its own guidance signals AI chip demand is still accelerating, not slowing, directly feeding the capital-spending wave at cloud and AI infrastructure companies, including Oracle.

Sales angle
When a prospect talks about "pausing" AI spending over cost concerns, numbers like TSMC's record quarter are a useful data point that overall enterprise and hyperscaler AI investment is still accelerating industry-wide, even if any one company's budget is tight.
SDR takeaway
"Advanced node" (7nm, 2nm, etc.) refers to the physical size of transistors on a chip — smaller nodes pack more computing power and efficiency into the same chip, and are the main hardware constraint behind today's AI compute shortages.

ASML Says Planned Chipmaking-Equipment Supply Won't Meet AI Demand Through 2028

ASML, the Dutch company whose lithography machines chipmakers like TSMC depend on, reported record third-quarter 2026 net sales of about €11.7 billion and net income of about €5.0 billion on October 14, and raised its full-year 2026 sales guidance to €44–45 billion. CEO Christophe Fouquet said customers are raising their equipment demand forecasts because of AI investment — some asking for orders stretching into 2029 and 2030 — and that ASML's already-planned 30% capacity increase for 2027 still won't be enough to meet demand in either 2027 or 2028. ASML has a near-monopoly on the extreme ultraviolet lithography machines needed to make the most advanced AI chips, so its statement that supply can't catch up to demand through 2028 signals that AI infrastructure shortages — and the capital spending they drive — will persist for years, not quarters.

Sales angle
This is useful, vendor-neutral evidence for conversations with prospects in hardware-adjacent industries about why AI-related equipment and cloud compute costs may stay elevated — it isn't one company's pricing decision, it's a multi-year supply constraint.
SDR takeaway
A single company controlling a critical step in a supply chain that every major chipmaker depends on is a textbook example of a supply-chain "chokepoint" — worth recognizing when prospects describe their own concentration risk with a key supplier.

Intuit Rolls Out "Intuit Intelligence" AI Agents for QuickBooks in Canada, UK, and Australia

On October 14, Intuit launched "Intuit Intelligence" for QuickBooks and Intuit Accountant Suite in Canada, part of a wider rollout that also covers the UK and Australia. Unlike a chatbot that only answers questions, the tools are built to complete tasks directly — drafting invoices from uploaded documents, chasing overdue payments, and flagging anomalies — while leaving final approval to the user. Intuit says early users save about 13 hours a month, though it also says the AI can make mistakes and still needs human review. QuickBooks is one of NetSuite's biggest competitors at the small-business end of the market, and this is Intuit pushing the same "agents that finish tasks, not just assist" positioning that Workday and Salesforce have also leaned into this month — a sign the whole ERP/accounting software category is converging on agentic AI as its core pitch.

Sales angle

Bank of America Beats on Revenue, but Profit Falls on a $1.2 Billion Settlement Charge

Bank of America reported third-quarter 2026 results on October 14: revenue of about $30 billion, up 3% year-over-year, but net income fell to $6.9 billion ($0.91 per diluted share) from $8.3 billion a year earlier, reflecting a $1.2 billion charge tied to a regulatory settlement. Adjusted earnings, which exclude that charge, were $8.1 billion ($1.08 per share). Net interest income rose 8% to $16.4 billion, and the bank's net charge-off ratio improved to 0.44%. Big-bank earnings are a widely watched read on the broader economy's health, and Bank of America's results show underlying lending and interest income staying strong even as a one-time legal charge drags down the headline profit number — a distinction worth knowing if a prospect cites "bank earnings" in either direction.

Most B2B Buyers Now Use AI to Research Vendors — But Almost All of Them Double-Check It

TrustRadius's 2026 B2B Buying Disconnect report, based on a January 2026 survey of 1,862 technology buyers and 444 vendors and released July 15, found that 63% of buyers used AI tools during their most recent purchase journey. Of those, 94% said they fact-check AI's answers at least some of the time. Product demos, free trials, and user reviews remain the most influential resources in the buying process; use of analyst reports has fallen to just 13% of buyers, down 63% since 2022. It's concrete evidence that AI chatbots have become a normal part of how buyers research vendors like NetSuite, but — contrary to a lot of AI-anxiety headlines — buyers are explicitly not taking AI's word at face value, which means the fundamentals of enterprise sales, like demos, trials, and real customer proof, still decide deals.

Sales angle
If a prospect mentions researching NetSuite or a competitor using ChatGPT or another AI tool, that's now the norm rather than a red flag — the real opening is making sure your own demos, trial experience, and customer references are strong enough to survive the fact-checking buyers are doing afterward.
SDR takeaway
Don't assume AI research replaces a demo or trial — the data says buyers increasingly start with AI but still verify through hands-on product experience before deciding, so the traditional sales motion still matters most at the end of the funnel.
