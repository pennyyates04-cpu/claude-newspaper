Oracle Weaves Ask Oracle and a New CPQ AI Assistant Into Core NetSuite

With the NetSuite 2026.2 release, now rolling out in phases through the third quarter, Oracle brought its "Ask Oracle" conversational AI assistant — previously limited to the NetSuite Next preview — into the core NetSuite product for U.S. and Canada accounts first. Ask Oracle lets users type plain-language questions to search records, generate reports, and complete tasks instead of building a saved search. The release also adds an AI Canvas workspace of AI-generated KPI scorecards and charts, a CPQ AI Assistant that helps reps configure complex quotes without manually cross-referencing pricing rules, and AI-assisted bank reconciliation. It's the clearest sign yet that Oracle is moving AI from an add-on to the default way people interact with NetSuite — the same shift Salesforce, SAP, and Workday are racing to make in their own platforms.

Salesforce Ships Seven Named Agentforce Agents, Citing 7 Billion Units of AI Work Done

On September 11, Salesforce announced seven "job-ready" Agentforce AI agents, each named and built for a specific function: Casey (customer service), Paige (IT/HR requests), Carter (commerce), Hunter (outbound sales prospecting), Marshall (supply chain and back-office automation), Piper (inbound pipeline), and Fin (customer experience). Six are generally available now; Hunter reaches general availability in November. Salesforce says it has delivered 7 billion "Agentic Work Units" — its internal metric for tasks agents complete — across Agentforce and Slack, with 3.2 billion of those in the most recent quarter alone. Naming and specializing agents by job function, rather than offering one generic assistant, is Salesforce's bet on how enterprises will actually adopt AI agents — as named "digital labor" with defined roles, not a single chatbot.

ServiceNow Acquires Israeli AI Startup Sweep, Its Sixth Israeli Deal of 2026

ServiceNow completed its acquisition of Sweep, an Israeli startup building what it calls an "agentic workspace" for go-to-market operations, in a deal reported to be worth hundreds of millions of dollars. Sweep's software monitors metadata across CRM and support platforms — including ServiceNow, Salesforce, and HubSpot — to flag misalignments and bottlenecks, generate documentation, and assist users in context. It's ServiceNow's sixth Israeli AI acquisition in 2026, following Pyramid Analytics, Veza, Traceloop, Armis (roughly $7.75 billion), and ai.work, bringing its total Israeli-company deal spend above $8 billion. ServiceNow is aggressively buying into the "agentic layer" that sits across multiple enterprise systems at once — a strategy that could make it a broader AI-orchestration competitor to CRM and ERP vendors, not just an IT service management vendor.

Salesforce Reaffirms $63 Billion Fiscal 2030 Revenue Target at Dreamforce, Beating Estimates

At Dreamforce on September 16, Salesforce COO/CFO Robin Washington reaffirmed a fiscal 2030 revenue target of more than $63 billion — above the $61.4 billion analysts had modeled, per Bloomberg data, and up from Salesforce's own "over $60 billion" framing as recently as October 2025. The reaffirmed target is Salesforce's direct answer to "SaaSpocalypse" concerns — the worry that AI agents doing work directly will shrink demand for traditional per-seat software licenses — arguing instead that AI (via Agentforce) will grow its revenue base.

Salesforce and Anthropic Detail Claudeforce, Merging Claude's Reasoning With Salesforce's Data Layer

Salesforce and Anthropic announced an expanded partnership called Claudeforce: Claude becomes a reasoning model available inside Salesforce, while Salesforce becomes a plugin inside Claude, with 37 prebuilt sales skills (meeting prep, deal-health review, pipeline review) that let sellers or agents act on live Salesforce data from within Claude, governed by Salesforce's existing permissions. Pilot customers have the plugin now; broader beta and more skills follow later in 2026. It's a notable example of a CRM giant partnering with, rather than only building, foundation-model AI — recognizing that customers increasingly want to work from within their AI assistant of choice, with enterprise data and guardrails following them there.

Sales angle
Prospects using Claude for research or drafting may soon be able to pull live CRM data into that same workflow — a preview of how AI assistants and core business systems like CRM and ERP could increasingly interoperate rather than compete.
SDR takeaway
A "reasoning model" plans and executes multi-step actions (like updating a record after research), not just answers questions — the same category Salesforce's own Koa model and this Claude integration both fall into.

Entrata's Updated IPO Filing Shows a Rare Combination: Fast Growth and GAAP Profitability

Entrata, a unified property-management software platform serving roughly 2.5 million U.S. multifamily housing units — about 10% of the market — updated its S-1 IPO filing with the SEC, showing first-half 2026 revenue of $294.6 million, up 23% year-over-year, and GAAP net income of $44.9 million for the period, following a full-year 2025 net income of $50.7 million on $509 million in revenue. The company plans to list on the NYSE under the ticker ENT. "GAAP" (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles) profitability combined with 20%+ growth is unusual for a software IPO candidate — most high-growth SaaS companies still post GAAP losses — making Entrata a closely watched test of investor appetite for profitable, still-growing vertical software.

New G2 Research: Nearly Half of B2B Software Buyers Had a CFO Veto an Approved Purchase

G2's 2026 Buyer Behavior Report, based on a survey of more than 1,000 B2B software buyers and interviews with over 50 sales and marketing leaders, found that 49% of buyers said a CFO vetoed an already-approved software purchase in the last 12 months — rising to 54% at organizations with a dedicated AI/LLM budget. Finance involvement in software buying jumped from 31% to 46% in a year. Partly as a result, 7 in 10 buyers say AI's pace is pushing them toward shorter contracts, and buyers who've faced a late-stage veto push for sub-12-month terms at more than double the rate of those who haven't (40% vs. 18%). It's hard evidence that finance teams, not just end users, have become active gatekeepers late in B2B software deals — a dynamic that can stall or kill deals that looked closed, and one pushing buyers toward shorter, lower-commitment contracts.

Sales angle
Directly relevant to closing NetSuite and other enterprise deals — getting finance (CFO/VP Finance) engaged early, rather than only at final approval, may reduce the risk of a late-stage veto derailing a deal that otherwise looked done.
SDR takeaway
When qualifying a deal, ask who on the finance side needs to sign off — a "yes" from a champion or department head is increasingly not the same as final approval.
